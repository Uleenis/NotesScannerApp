# Architecture

The detailed contracts of the code: what each part does, its bounds, its answers and its refusals. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the map and the rules; read here the section of the part a branch touches, and update it in the same branch when a contract changes.

## Layout in detail

- `src/app/`: routes (Expo Router) and their `_layout.tsx`, nothing else.
- `src/components/`: shared components.
- `src/i18n/`: language files (`en.ts`, `fr.ts`) and the choice of language.
- `src/auth/`: the anonymous account, the provider that opens it when the app starts and follows every change of account (sign-up, sign-in, sign-out), and the actions of the account by e-mail code (`account.ts`, `account-errors.ts`, `account-service.ts`). A sign-in from an anonymous account that has pages checks its code on a second client that keeps nothing (`getStatelessSupabase`), calls `transfer-account` with both tokens, and only then gives the account found to the client of the phone.
- `src/lib/`: the Supabase client, its config, the secure session storage, the addresses of the terms and the privacy policy (`legal-links.ts`), the opening of a web address (`open-link.ts`) and the forgetting of the data of other accounts.
- `src/notebooks/`: reading, creating and caching notebooks.
- `src/capture/`: the limits shown before the scanner and the hook that opens the capture screen or the limit sheet, sending a page (create, prepare, upload, mark it uploaded, start the reading), preparing the photo, the camera and network hooks. The photo is saved again in the sense of the viewfinder, never in the sense the phone guessed from gravity: the camera gives its orientation tag (`exif: true`), the app turns the image back to the viewfinder (plus the quarter turns of "Rotate"), shrinks it above 12 Mpx, and saves one JPEG with no metadata. The viewfinder and the check of the photo have the shape of the photo (3:4). A page only counts for the user (notebook counts, last notebook, page lists) once its `uploaded_at` is set, so a page created for a photo that never arrived stays invisible. The `source` of a page is `camera` or `import` (slice 6, `t6/source`): the check `pages_source_check` refuses any other value, exactly as written, the client sets it with the insert of the page and has no right to change it afterwards, and `insertPage` takes it as its last argument (`camera` by default). The system scanner (slice 6, `t6/dev-build`) is reached through `scanner.ts` alone: `scanPages({ limit })` refuses a limit that is not a whole number from 1 to 20 (`RangeError`, the scanner stays closed), asks the native module for that many pages with the gallery off, and answers `scanned` with the files in the order the user chose, `cancelled` (no page), `unavailable` (no native module, or the phone has no scanner: an Android without Google Play services) or `failed`; an answer that is not a list of `file:///…/scans/<name>.jpg` addresses (letters, digits and dashes in the name), or holds more pages than the limit, is `failed` and the scanned files it named are erased, never any other file. `scanLimit(remaining)` is the smaller of the pages left and 20. `scanForMeasure()` opens the scanner with the gallery on, 30 pages at most, and only when `__DEV__` is true; the measurement screen (`src/app/measure.tsx`, reached from a row of the account screen shown only in development builds, and sent back to the library otherwise) shares the pages as one ZIP of stored entries named `01.jpg`, `02.jpg`… through the share sheet, in an `export-` folder of the cache left to the export sweep, erases the scanned files, and uploads nothing. The sharpness check (slice 6, `t6/sharpness`, `sharpness.ts`) is pure TypeScript run on the phone before sending, with no server and no model: `judgeSharpness` takes a grayscale image (`Gray`: bytes, width, height; it refuses with a `RangeError` a size that is not whole numbers from 1, above 12 Mpx, or that does not match the bytes), reduces it to 1,000 px on its long side (never enlarged; a box average of the source pixels whose centres fall in the window of each output pixel, as Pillow's `BOX` does), and divides the variance of the 3 by 3 Laplacian (0, 1, 0 / 1, −4, 1 / 0, 1, 0, edges replicated) by the variance of the pixels, so that the faint ink of a pencil page does not look blurred. It answers `blurry` below the threshold 0.55, else `sharp` (a flat or blank page scores 0 and is `blurry`). Measure 16, redone in branch 4 on the 21 paper photographs of the scanner and the 21 scans of the iPad (`04 Testing/T6 mesures/crop/sharp.py` and `sharp2.py`): the plain variance of the Laplacian with the old threshold 300 flagged 6 of the 21 paper photographs, and no threshold of it met criterion 16 (at most 87.5 % of the blurred versions for all 42 sharp images passing); with the contrast divided out, the worst sharp image scores 0.618, 0.55 passes the 42 and flags 166 of the 168 blurred versions (98.8 %), and the TypeScript function gives the same scores as the kit on the same photos (0.620 against 0.621). The threshold is still to be checked with the phone's own reduction and decoding (criterion 18). `captureService.checkSharpness(uri)` renders the photo, reduces it to 1,000 px with the image manipulator, saves a copy as a JPEG at full quality, decodes it with `jpeg-js` (`decodeJpegGray`: 2 Mpx and 64 MB at most, no tolerance for a broken file; gray = 0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B), judges it and erases the copy; it answers `sharp`, `blurry` or `unchecked` (a file that cannot be decoded or any error: a page is never signalled as blurry on a failure of the check). The fixtures are synthetic (`tests/fixtures/synthetic-page.ts`: generated strokes, Gaussian and motion blurs at the levels of measure 16); no page of the corpus enters the repository. The capture screen (slice 6, `t6/series`, `src/app/capture.tsx` and the hook `use-series.ts`) opens the system scanner at once, with `seriesLimit(allowance)` pages at most (the smaller of the pages left and 20; 20 when the limits are unknown, never below 1, so that the server stays the authority), and keeps the notebook of the destination (changeable once the scanner is closed), the counter and a `Scan` button. A closed scanner creates nothing; `unavailable` says so and offers no button; `failed` says so and offers the button again. The `Cancel` button is always there, so a scanner that never answers cannot hold the screen, and a result that arrives after the screen is gone is erased, never sent. Each scanned file is saved again by `captureService.prepareScan` (one JPEG without metadata, shrunk above 12 Mpx), the scanner's file is erased, and `checkSharpness` runs on the prepared one (`prepareScans` in `series.ts`: a page that cannot be prepared is left out and counted, a check that throws is `unchecked`). If a page is `blurry`, nothing is sent: the list of blurry pages shows each with `Retake` (the scanner for one page, which replaces the page at the same place and is checked again; a closed scanner changes nothing) and `Send anyway`; with no blurry page left the series goes. `sendSeries` sends the pages one at a time in order, through the usual `sendPage` (create, prepare, upload, mark uploaded, start the reading), so the next page is created after the reading of the previous one is started and the positions follow; a sent page's file is erased. A page the server cannot read (`photo`) or that is too heavy is left out and counted, and the series goes on; a limit, a lost network or any other failure stops it at once with the pages not sent, the half-sent page keeping its page id so that a new try creates nothing twice. A limit shows how many pages were not sent and the reason (the pages not sent are erased; a refusal on the first page opens the limit sheet as for one page); a lost network keeps the pages in the cache with `Try again` (waiting while offline). Leaving with files still held asks "The N pages not sent will be lost" (Cancel button, hardware back included), then erases them and stops the series after the page in flight. Once everything went, one page opens the page, two or more open the chapter of the destination (the notebook when the pages are in no chapter), and a series with pages left out ends on a message first. `sweepScans()` erases the `scans`, `ImageManipulator` and `ImagePicker` folders of the cache at every start of the app (a page scanned before the app was killed has no other way out; the iOS `tmp/scans` folder, out of reach of `expo-file-system`, is left to the pre-beta). The import from the gallery (slice 6, `t6/import`, `importer.ts`) is the second way into the same series: the idle screen offers `Import photos` beside `Scan` (alone when the scanner is `unavailable`, with the scan again when it `failed`). `pickImages({ limit })` refuses a limit that is not a whole number from 1 to 20 (`RangeError`), then opens the system photo picker of `expo-image-picker` for images only, several at once up to the limit, in the order chosen, with no editing, no EXIF and no Base64 (never the legacy picker, which reaches files outside the photo library; no permission is asked, the picker gives the files the user chose). It answers `picked` with the accepted files and the number set aside, `cancelled` (nothing chosen) or `failed` (the picker threw, answered an odd shape, or more files than the limit: every file of the picker's folder it named is erased). The closed case: an address is accepted only if it starts with the `ImagePicker/` folder of the app cache (`Paths.cache`) and the rest is `<letters, digits and dashes>.<2 to 5 letters or digits>`, so no `..`, `%` escape, newline or other folder passes (anything else is set aside and never erased, so a hostile address cannot make the app delete another file); the same address twice is taken once; the file must be 1 byte to 64 MB; its format is read from its first bytes, never from its name or the type the system reported, and only JPEG, PNG, WebP and HEIC are on the allow-list (a PDF, a GIF, a video, a text are set aside); the pixel count is bounded before anything decodes the image, at 50 Mpx and 16,000 px on a side: for a JPEG by its header (`jpegSize` of `src/pages/photo-pixels.ts` with these bounds in place of those of the reading service, so a 65,535 by 65,535 file of a few kilobytes is refused), for a PNG by its `IHDR`, for a WebP or a HEIC by the size the system reported, which must be whole, positive and within the bounds (a JPEG or a PNG ignores the reported size, the header is the authority). Once the scanner has answered `unavailable`, the idle screen keeps saying so and offering only the import until the screen is left, whatever happens to the import; a replacement import that yields nothing says so on the blurry sheet and keeps the page. A file set aside is erased, counted, and said at the end by the same sentence as a page left out. Each accepted image is then shown one at a time (`Page 2 of 5`) by `PhotoReview`, its `Retake` button becoming `Remove`: `captureService.prepareImport(original, quarterTurns)` turns the original by the quarter turns of `Rotate` (always from the original, so four turns come back to the first position) and saves one JPEG without metadata, shrunk above 12 Mpx, which is what the review shows; an image that cannot be prepared is left out, counted and erased, and a turn that fails is said and leaves the image as it was. Preparation is lazy, one image at a time as the user reaches it. `Keep this page` erases the original and keeps the prepared file; `Remove` erases both. After the last image, `checkImports` runs `checkSharpness` on the kept files (same rules as `prepareScans`: a check that throws is `unchecked`), then the same blurry-page sheet and `sendSeries` as a scanned series, with `source = import` given to `sendPage` and so to `insertPage`; `Retake` on a blurry imported page opens the picker for one image, reviewed the same way, which replaces the page at the same place. Leaving during the review asks the same neutral question with the number of pages that would be lost, then erases the originals and the prepared files; a picker that answers after the screen is gone has its files erased, never prepared nor sent. Everything else of the series (limit, network, destination, cache) is unchanged. The writing test keeps its own camera (`SheetPhoto`).
- `src/pages/`: the pages a user sees (list, one page with its number and text, signed address of its photo, starting or restarting a reading), what a page shows according to its status, the title of a page and the gluing of its cut lines at display time (the text itself is never changed), `clean.ts` (the one module that cleans a page of text for display: it removes `<u>` and `</u>` and no other tag, and a blank line between two non-empty lines of B1 unless B2, read by `second.ts` from `readings` with `label = 'B2'`, has a blank line between the lines that match them by content without case, accents, spaces or punctuation; a line without a match keeps its blank line, nothing is removed without B2, on a page that has gaps written by an edit, on a page of math, or above 1,000,000 pairs of lines; it only returns a text and the words moved to it, so no reading, place or position changes, and the Edit mode starts from that text and writes nothing when it is left unchanged), dates without `Intl`, and the polling delays. Every query here keeps to pages whose `uploaded_at` is set. `formulas.ts` cuts the body of a page of math into text and formulas (`$…$` on one line with no space inside its dollars, `$$…$$`, `\(…\)`, `\[…\]`, `\begin{…}…\end{…}`; anything unclosed stays text) and into rows; `tex.ts` draws one formula as an SVG string with MathJax 4 (`@mathjax/src` and its font, Apache-2.0) on the phone, with the TeX packages `base` and `ams` only (no `\href`, `\url`, `\class`, `\style`, `\require`), every font range it may need imported with the app so nothing is ever fetched, and an output with a link or an image refused; it refuses before drawing a formula over 2,000 characters, nested deeper than 20 (counted with what is open: a brace, a `\left` until its `\right`, an environment until its `\end`), using `\DeclareMathOperator` or `\pmb` (their nesting doubles the work), `\mmlToken` (MathML attributes), `\newcolumntype`, `\label`, `\tag`, `\ref`, `\eqref`, `\hsize`, `\vbox`, `\vtop`, `\vcenter` or `\parbox` (the last five start a slow line breaking), an environment outside the common ones (the matrices, `cases`, `aligned`, `align`, `gathered`, `gather`, `equation`, `split`, `multline`, `flalign`, `eqnarray`, `array`; so no `alignat` nor `subarray`), an `array` whose column template is not 1 to 20 columns `l`, `c` or `r` with at most one `|` around each, a `\begin` whose name cannot be read, or a `%` comment (it would hide what it closes from the count); after drawing it refuses an SVG over 512 KB, larger than 300 by 120 ex or 4,000 square ex, of more than 3,000 elements, or holding a link, an image or a `url(`. Each formula gets a fresh TeX input and a fresh SVG output, so nothing it defines or sets reaches the next; the drawing must be one root picture (inline line breaks are off). Formulas are drawn one at a time, each after a turn of the screen, a formula asked twice waits for the first drawing, a drawing still queued when its formula leaves the screen is dropped (`releaseTex`), a page draws at most 100 formulas and shows the rest as read, and the last 50 results are kept (the oldest goes first) and read at once. A formula it refuses is shown as it was read, never as an error. `words.ts` reads the positions (`page_tokens`) and corrections of a page by pages of 1,000 rows (20 reads at most, else the page is not editable) and places each word in the text the page shows: from the places in B1 (`b1_start`, `b1_end`, in characters) shifted by the length of every word a correction or a rule changed, checked against the text (a changed word that is not where it should be, a word without a place, overlapping places or a place past the end make the whole page not editable, never a guess); the suggestions of a word are its distinct readings of A, B1 and B2, in that order. `isWord` is the client's copy of the word rule of `corrections` (1 to 100 characters, none of the fixed list of spaces, controls and invisible characters), and `addCorrection` sends only `page_id`, `token_index`, `corrected_text`, `chosen_from` and `from_queue: false`, and nothing when the word fails `isWord`. `displayPieces` (`display.ts`) gives the same title and blocks as `displayDocument` as pieces that each know their place in the text, so a word can be found in the glued lines. The engine itself (`mathjax-engine.ts`) is loaded on the first formula, not at app start. On a page of math the first line is the title only when no formula starts on it; display formulas scroll sideways when wider than the screen, and an inline formula wider than 36 ex is shown as read. `mathjax-context.ts`, imported first, gives React Native's `navigator` the `appVersion` and `userAgent` MathJax reads at load. `metro.config.js` resolves the package subpath imports of MathJax (`#default-font/*` and kin), whose bare targets Metro 0.84 cannot follow. `edit.ts` is the "Modifier" mode: `alignEdit` takes the text shown, the placed words and the text the user wrote and answers `same`, `refused` (a text over 100,000 characters, more than 1,000 differences between the two lists of words, or more than 2,000 words changed) or the words and gaps to send; it matches the words of the two texts with the word rule of the service (the same regular expression, combining marks counted as letters) by a Myers difference, exact word for exact word (a position whose word holds a character outside that rule, kept by an earlier correction, is looked for whole in the new text first), pairs in order the words that changed inside a hole of the difference (a position left without a word is deleted, a word left without a position stays in the text between the words), keeps every other character (added words, line cuts, punctuation, spaces) as the text of the user in the gaps, and checks that the words and gaps give the text exactly back; `editPage` calls `edit-page`. `words.ts` reads `page_gaps` too (`gaps` in `PageWords`) and `placeWords` places each word after the gap that comes before it, a deleted word having no length (never tappable). `alerts.ts` reads the kinds of alert a page shows (`missing_line` on a page of text, `math_incomplete` on a page of math). `use-text-edit.ts` holds the state of the edit: the draft, the problem (`tooBig`, `changed`, `refused`, `failed`) and the save.
- `src/search/`: the search (slice 7, `t7/search-screen`). `search.ts` calls `search_pages` and `suggest_words` together with the trimmed query and the notebook filter (or null) and gives `found` with its hits (page, notebook, rank 1 or 2, position, excerpt, the marked range in code points, the form also read) and its suggestions, or `tooLong` / `tooManyWords` for the two refusals `22023`, or `error` for any other failure or any row it cannot read; a failure of the suggestion alone is no failure. `readAnchor` reads `anchor_kind` and `anchor` of one position of `page_tokens` with the rights of the user and gives nothing for a box that is not four numbers. `text.ts` cuts an excerpt on its marked range (code points to UTF-16) and replaces the query words the suggestion named (compared lowercased without accents, a part of a hyphenated word included). `found.ts` accepts a position only as 1 to 9 digits. `service.ts` holds the two calls, `queries.ts` the hooks (the cache key `search` is invalidated with the pages), `timing.ts` the 400 ms wait before the field searches by itself. The screen `src/app/search.tsx` takes an optional `notebook` parameter (ignored unless it is one of the user's notebooks): field of 100 characters at most, one chip per notebook, results under « In the text » (rank 1) and « Also read » (rank 2), the word in bold at rank 1, the form read at rank 2, one tap to search the suggestion. A result opens `/page/[id]?found=<position>`: `FoundPhoto` (in the document of a page of text) reads the box of that position, loads the photo in a hidden 1-pixel probe, reads the size of the photo from the header of its JPEG (`src/pages/photo-pixels.ts`: a `Range` request on its first 128 KiB, the size of the frame marker, swapped for EXIF orientations 5 to 8, nothing outside 8,000 pixels a side or 12 million pixels, nothing for a header it cannot read, a failed request is an error so that it is tried again), never from the size the image reports when it loads (Android reduces an image over 2,048 pixels when it loads it and reports the reduced size), and shows the photo (at most 320 high) with the box only when the box lies on the photo (`frameOf` in `src/pages/frame.ts`: four finite numbers, the right and bottom edges at most 2 pixels past the photo, none before 0, the box not empty); otherwise the page opens on its text alone. A tap on that photo opens the photo sheet with the same box (`/page/[id]/photo?found=<position>`), drawn inside the zoomed layer. The box is in the pixels of the stored photo `original.jpg`, which the service reads as it is (the app saved it upright with no metadata).
- `src/export/`: the export of a page, a chapter or a notebook as a PDF or a Markdown file (slice 7, `t7/export`), shared through the system sheet. `document.ts` plans an export from the screen's pages (`planPage`, `planChapter`, `planNotebook`: only read pages that have a text and are not in an unsupported script, chapters in screen order, pages newest first inside each section, pages outside a chapter last under their heading) and builds the document: the title and blocks of `displayDocument` over `cleanView` (tags removed, blank lines of B1 dropped unless B2 shows them, skipped for a page edited by the user), or `mathDocument` rows for a page of math. `sources.ts` reads the B2 text of the pages (groups of 50) and, one page at a time (6 in parallel), whether a page has a gap or a deleted word, with the rights of the user. `markdown.ts` writes one paragraph per group of lines (a hard break between lines, a blank line at a gap), every text escaped (`\ ` * _ [ ] < > & ~ | $ #`, the marker of a list or heading at the start of a line, no indentation, no raw HTML or entity ever); a formula keeps its LaTeX between `$` only when it passes `acceptable` and, once its white space is folded to single spaces, holds only letters, digits and the characters `space { } ^ _ = + - * / ( ) . , | : ; ! ? ' < > & and the backslash`, no address (`:/`, `www.`) and no trailing lone backslash (`<` and `>` become `\lt` and `\gt`), otherwise its source is escaped text. `html.ts` embeds at most 8 million characters of drawings (the rest is escaped source) and encodes each distinct drawing once. It writes the PDF's HTML from fixed templates and escaped text only (`&`, `<`, `>`, quotes), with a content security policy `default-src 'none'; img-src data:; style-src 'unsafe-inline'`, no script, no link, no font, no outside resource; a formula is an `img` whose source is the drawing of `tex.ts` percent-encoded as `data:image/svg+xml;utf8,...` (an image runs nothing), sized by finite numbers, or its escaped source in `code` when it was not drawn. `run.ts` is the one flow: nothing to export, over 100 pages for a PDF or 500 for Markdown (`tooMany`), read the sources, build, draw each distinct formula in order (at most 100 formulas a page and 300 an export, the rest stays text), refuse a finished text over 12 million characters for a PDF or 20 million for Markdown (`tooLarge`), save, share; the file is erased at once when nothing was shared, and otherwise left to the share target (Android resolves the share when the chooser closes, a target may read later) until the next export or the next start of the app sweeps every `export-*` folder of the cache; a `cancelled` control stops it between steps, including the reads of `sources.ts`, and an error after a cancel is a cancel. Any other error writes one `export_failed` line to the development log (`console.warn`) with the step (`sources`, `renderings`, `content`, `save`, `share`), the name and the code of the error when they are short identifiers, never its message, a text, a file name or a path. `files.ts` writes into a new folder of the cache (`export-<random>`): the Markdown file, or the PDF of `expo-print` (A4, 595 by 842, printed a second time when the first print fails) written under its name from the base64 data the print returns, never moved: in Expo Go the print lands in the root cache folder, outside the folders the app may move from (`ERR_INVALID_PERMISSION`), so the printed file is only erased when the system allows it; a failure is named `PrintFailed` or `WriteFailed` for the log; the file name is the name of the page, chapter or notebook reduced by `exportFileName` to letters, digits, dot, dash and underscore, 80 characters at most, never starting or ending on a dot, dash or underscore, and at most 200 bytes of UTF-8 (combining marks are kept). `activity.ts` and `components/export-host.tsx` hold the one running export and its wait with a Cancel button (Android back too); `use-export.ts` gives the menu item « Exporter » (PDF or Markdown) of the three menus, and an Alert for `empty`, `tooMany`, `unavailable` and `error`.
- `src/render/`: the rich rendering on the page (slice 9, `t9/screen`). `markdown.ts` parses the Markdown of a stored rendering (60,000 characters at most, else `null`) into blocks of an allow-list: headings 1 to 3, items of two levels (bullets or the number as written; a second level with no item before it is read as a first one), paragraphs, display formulas, and spans of text with bold, italic, or an inline formula. It applies the rule of `checkFidelity` (`render-check.ts`): a unit is a paragraph, a list item with its continuation lines or a heading; a blank line, a line that starts with a mark or a heading closes it; inside a unit the same three global passes (`**`, `*`, `_`, in that order) remove the emphasis marks and flag what they wrap, so the client shows the words and signs the server compared and nothing else (`tests/render-markdown-server.test.ts` compares both on random inputs and times hostile ones). Everything else (HTML, links, images, code, tables, quotes, `####`, escapes) is shown as text; formulas are masked first with placeholder characters picked among the private-use ones the text does not hold. `api.ts` has `renderPage` (calls `render-page` with the page alone: `ready` with its text, `limit`, `failed`, `discarded`, `network` or `error`), `readRendering` and `readRenderings` (50 pages a read) over `page_renderings`, and `renderingFingerprint` (the SHA-256 of the stored text without `<u>` tags, written in `sha256.ts`, equal to the server's). `mode.ts` keeps the choice "faithful" or "enriched" in `expo-secure-store` (key `render-mode`, faithful when unknown). `use-rendering.ts` decides when to call: only when the page asks for the rendering, its words are placed and the stored rendering is not current; one call at most per account, page and fingerprint while the app runs, a failure is remembered (`discarded` for good, the others until the user chooses "enriched" again) and the faithful text stays shown with one neutral sentence. The switch (`render-switch.tsx`) and the body (`rich-body.tsx`) are in `src/components/reading/`; touching the text, "Edit" or switching the type of the page returns that page to the faithful text without changing the saved choice. The export (`src/export/`) uses the saved choice: in the enriched mode it reads the stored renderings of the pages (never calls the model) and writes a page from its rendering only when the rendering is current, with the headings relative to the title of the page.
- `src/export/data/`: the export of "my data" from the account screen (slice 7, `t7/my-data`), assembled on the phone with the rights of the user: no function, no signed address beyond one photo at a time. `read.ts` reads an allow-list of columns (`EXPORTED_COLUMNS`, checked against `my-data-scope.test.sql` by `export-data-columns.test.ts`), 1,000 rows a request (`range`, at most 200,000 rows a table), always with `user_id = the user` (`id` for `profiles`) and, for the seven page tables, `page_id in` the group asked for, and drops any row of another page. `archive.ts` plans (`planMyData`: the pages, at most 10,000, and the size estimated from the folders of at most 8 pages in `photos`, scaled to the page count; the two photos of each writing test are not counted, the margin of 50 MB covers them) and writes (`writeMyData`): refuses to start without free space for 1.2 times the estimate plus 50 MB (`noSpace`); then `README.txt` (English then French), `profile.json` (with the year of birth and the country), `usage.json` (allowance and charges), `notebooks.json` (notebooks, chapters, lexicon), the photos of the writing tests that still have them (`writing-tests/<test>/1.jpg` and `2.jpg`, for a test `pending` or `accepted`; a test id that is not a UUID stops the export; a photo that is gone is listed with `file: null` and counted like a page photo) and `training.json` (the `consents` with their parent address, the `writing_tests` with the photos listed, the `models` without `storage_path` and `sha256`: three allow-listed tables, `consents`, `writing_tests` and `htr_models`, read with `user_id = the user`), then the pages 20 at a time (the seven page tables are read once per group): the photos of the page (`original`, `prepared`; one that is gone from storage is listed with `file: null` and counted in `export.json`, any other failure stops the export), `text.txt`, `page.json` (the page row, the pipeline version, the readings B1, B2 and A with model, versions and budget, the positions, the corrections, the text added by hand, the alerts, the photo files), and last `export.json`. A photo is fetched only when its path starts with `{user_id}/{page_id}/`. `zip.ts` writes the archive with `fflate` into one file handle, entry by entry (text deflated, photos stored in 256 KB chunks), refuses any name outside its allow-list or twice, and stops with `ArchiveTooLargeError` past 3.5 GB. `service.ts` binds the real parts: a photo is downloaded from a signed address (10 minutes) into a folder `export-photo-*`, must be a JPEG of at most 12 MB, and is erased once written; the archive folder is `export-*`; the sheet is the system's (`application/zip`). `use-my-data.ts` runs the flow: wait, plan, an Alert with the pages and the approximate size, wait with a progress bar (`ExportHost`, which the account screen also embeds because it is a modal) and a Cancel button, then the sheet. A is written as `medium`, version `1`, without a model, or as `personal`, the version of the model, with its id. `src/reading/rules.py` holds the rules, gate open, in the order 2, 5, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 of the Arbitrage (rule 1 waits for slice 5): the type is the strictest of A, B1 and B2 (a digit in any makes a number; a proper noun flagged by the entities port on B1, on the word of A paired with it or on the word of B2 paired with it); readings are compared in their form (case kept, apostrophes and dashes unified); rule 2 (number read differently) writes B1 to `queue`; rule 5 (B1 and B2 differ and A confirms one of them) writes the word A confirms, as that pass read it, to `layer`; rule 3 (proper noun) B1 to `layer`; rule 4 (common word, A at a confidence of at least 0.990, one edit, homophones by the phonemes port, not an accent alone, not a truncation) A on a `journal` notebook, else B1, to `layer`; rule 6 (B1 and B2 differ and A confirms neither, or read nothing there) B1 to `queue`; rule 7 (the word is on a line B2 lacks) B1 to `none`, the alert being the page's; rule 8 (any other disagreement) B1 to `layer`; rule 9 (A and B1 agree, whatever B2 reads) and rule 10 (A read nothing there) B1 to `none`. Without B2, rules 5, 6 and 7 stay silent. `SecondRefusedError` is raised when the entities port refuses B2 (over 15,000 sub-tokens): the arbiter then decides the page without B2. Language tiers: French runs the entities and rule 4 in French; English, rule 4 in English and no entities; any other language, or none found, neither. `src/reading/phonemes.py` is espeak-ng through `phonemizer` (`espeakng-loader`, `fr-fr` and `en-us`, no stress, `language_switch="remove-utterance"`: a word espeak reads in another language has no phonemes and is no homophone), one call at a time, logging nothing. `src/reading/replay.py` (`python -m reading.replay` in the image) replays readings through the rules with the gate forced open: JSON on stdin (pages with B1, optionally B2, A's lines of words and confidences, language, level, and the entity flags of B1, of A and, with B2, of B2 pinned), positions on stdout; a bad input exits 2 with `invalid replay input`, never its content. The arbiter reads the correction level of the page's notebook only when the gate is open. `src/reading/entities.py` is the entities port: `distilcamembert-base-ner` in ONNX on the processor, one thread, read in windows of 510 sub-tokens on the form of the text (as the NER Spike did), `<` and `>` and their full-width and small-form look-alikes read as spaces, and any special id left after encoding read as `<unk>`, so that no special token can be written on a page, and above 15,000 sub-tokens for B1 or for A the arbitration answers 422 `too_complex`; a word is a proper noun when the first sub-token over its characters carries `PER`, `LOC`, `ORG` or `MISC`; one flag per token of `line_tokens`. onnxruntime 1.30 sends telemetry to Microsoft unless `ORT_DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` is set before it loads (the API switch comes too late): the image sets it, `Camembert` refuses to start without it and imports onnxruntime only after that check, and a test proves no connection opens. The service wires the rules at start (`Espeak` and `Camembert`) and does not start without `READING_NER`, the folder of the NER files. `src/reading/language.py` finds the language of B1 with lingua (all its languages, minimum relative distance 0.25), none under five words with a letter. `src/reading/second.py` ranges B2 by content before the aligner: `b2_content_map` of the Pairing Probe, same steps over the forms (case kept, apostrophes and dashes unified), then the global aligner of `Disagreement Spike/scripts/tokens.py` (`align_global`), so that each word of B1 has the word of B2 read there, or none; on 399 random pages and on the 52 stored pairs of real readings of the spikes it pairs exactly as the spike. A line of B1 (or of B2) is missing when none of its words is paired and its run of words is nowhere in the other pass. B2 is left out when it has no word, when it passes 1,000,000 cells or 50,000,000 letter pairs with B1, or when the search passes 20,000,000 steps (a page of identical words): the page is read as without B2, never refused. `src/reading/tokens.py` also gives `raw_spans`, the place of each word in the characters of the text as it was written (the NFC form `tokens_of` reads can be shorter); none for the whole page when a word would end inside a composed character. `src/reading/arbiter.py` reads the user's models, computes the positions and the language, gives each position its place in B1 (`b1_start`, `b1_end`) and the word B2 read there (`reading_b2`), gate open or not, and raises the `missing_line` alert of the page when one pass lacks a whole line (`absent_from_b2`, `absent_from_b1`: the indexes of those lines, 50 at most each, no text), gate open or not, uploads the ALTO to `alto/{user_id}/{page_id}/a-<32 hex>.xml` (a new name for each attempt), then calls `record_reading_a` (model `medium`, version `1`, the alerts in `p_alerts` only when there are some). It erases that file when the write is refused (409 with `page_not_found`, `not_text` or `already_read`) or surely failed (400, 401, 403, 404 or 409); after a failure that may have written (network, timeout, any other status) it leaves the file and logs `alto=left`. `src/reading/store.py` is the only code that calls Supabase: `htr_models`, the `correction_level` of a page's notebook (read through `pages`), the `alto` bucket and `record_reading_a`, with the service key, never `page_texts`, over https only (http only to `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `::1` or the stack's `supabase_kong_*` gateway, a name without a dot), following no redirect and no proxy of the environment. It answers 200 with `page_id`, `outcome` (`recorded`) and `tokens`, or 400 `invalid_request`, 409, 413 `too_large` (above 12 MB), 415, 422 `too_complex`, 503 `busy` (four arbitrations in flight), 500 `arbitration_failed`. Logs carry the page, durations and counts, never text or image. `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` pin every package: the Kraken chain keeps the versions that produced the Cold Spike (`constraint-dependencies`), torch comes from the PyTorch index for the processor. The `Dockerfile` downloads the `medium` model from Zenodo (record 21788410, Apache-2.0) and checks its SHA-256, and the NER the same way from Hugging Face at pinned revisions: `model.onnx` and `config.json` of `cmarkea/distilcamembert-base-ner` (MIT), `tokenizer.json` of `Xenova/camembert-ner` (the CamemBERT tokenizer the NER Spike used), into `/models/ner`; Kraken carries its `blla` segmenter; the service runs as a user that is not root and cannot change its model. Every route requires the shared secret in the `X-Reading-Secret` header, compared in constant time, and `Authorization` stays free for the identity token of Cloud Run; without a `READING_SECRET` of at least 32 characters the service does not start. Nor does it start without `SUPABASE_URL` (as above) and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`. `check.sh` runs ruff, mypy, pytest and pip-audit inside the image; `audit-allowlist.txt` lists the advisories pip-audit ignores. Both are guarded, and CI calls `check.sh` by name, so the default command of the image cannot skip it. `compose.yaml` runs it read-only with `/tmp` not executable; espeak copies its library into `READING_ESPEAK_DIR`, a small tmpfs of its own that allows it (`/run/espeak`); it runs on the network of the stack, at `http://notes-reading:8080`, and on `127.0.0.1:54340` for the PC only. Its local secret lives in `services/reading/.env`, ignored by git. `POST /check-writing?template=fr|en&sheet=1|2` (slice 5, `t5/writing-test`; same secret, same four places as `/recognize`) reads a JPEG of one sheet with the generic model and answers only `{template, sheet, lines, training, validation}`. `src/reading/writing_test.py` joins the words of each line read, aligns the read text with the text of the sheet character by character (unit costs, one dynamic-programming table, a line break of the read text matching a line break of the model), cuts the text of the sheet at the line breaks of the read text, and keeps a read line when its edit distance to its piece over the length of the piece is at most 0.6; a kept line covers the lines of the text of the sheet its piece spans; the answer counts the distinct lines of the text covered by kept pieces that end before line 31 of the test (training) or start at line 31 or later (validation), and a piece that straddles the two counts for neither, so a line of the text read as two lines counts once and a missing line is never made up. More than 60 lines or 4,000 characters read is `too_complex`. Only numbers are answered and logged. The trainer of slice 5 (`t5/trainer`) lives in the same package and is never started by the service: `src/reading/harvest.py` ports `Loop Spike/scripts/harvest.py` with the rule A = B1 to the character, case kept (the word with apostrophes and hyphens removed, as the spike's `tokenize(keep_case=True)`), without B2: the page is read again into tiles, B1 is aligned with that reading by the block aligner (lowercase forms, each hole worth as many of B1's longest lines as it holds), and each line gives its longest run of words whose tokens all agree with B1 and follow each other in B1 (a word of B1 that the reading skipped cuts the run); a corrected position counts as agreeing with the text of its last correction when the reading has a word there, except in a language other than French or English, or when the correction is a homophone one edit from the reading of A (espeak); the tile is cut half-way into the gap with each neighbouring word, and a cut under 8 px is dropped. `src/reading/training.py` holds the settings of the spikes (`ketos train` from `medium.safetensors`, `--resize new`, `ppocrv6 medium`, `--max-width 1600`, `-B 2`, `--augment`, `-r 0.0003`, `--warmup 200`, `-N 120`, `-q fixed`, `bf16-mixed`), the CER of `ketos test` (errors over characters of its report), the cut of the test sheets as `writing_test.py` does (a kept line becomes a training sample when it ends before line 31, a validation sample when it starts at 31 or later, with the text as printed), the count of planted faults a model writes in their correct form, and the checks of a model file: a `.safetensors` file, never a symbolic one, whose header parses, its SHA-256 checked, and a checkpoint converted only when it is a `checkpoint_*.ckpt` of the task's own folder. `src/reading/trainer.py` (`python -m reading.trainer`) holds only `SUPABASE_URL` (https, or http to the local stack) and `TRAINING_SECRET`, never the service key; it refuses to start unless `medium.safetensors` has its fingerprint; it claims a task every minute, reads strictly the answer (addresses under `/storage/v1/object/sign/photos/` or `/models/`, nothing else; an answer it cannot read is reported `failed` when its task id can be read), downloads to a folder of the task (photos 10 MB, model 100 MB at most, a photo opened by `open_photo`), reads the test and up to 300 pages with the active model (or the generic one), stops reading pages 40 minutes after the claim (the addresses live one hour) or at 20,000 samples, frees the graphics memory of that reader, trains for 60 minutes at most and never past 80 minutes after the claim (20 minutes are kept for the measures and the upload; with less than 10 minutes left the task fails), keeps the best weights by their score or, when interrupted, converts the best checkpoint of its own folder, measures the new and the base model on the validation lines, uploads to the address `training-upload` gives for its own task id only, reports, and erases the task folder whatever happens. `services/training/` builds that package with every locked package checked by its hash, then torch 2.14.0 for CUDA 13.0 and its CUDA libraries from the PyTorch index alone (`TORCH_CUDA`, `cu130` by default; an NVIDIA driver 580 or later) and the `medium` model checked by its SHA-256; it is not built in CI. Its `compose.yaml` runs it on the network of the local stack with the GPU, read-only, no port published, the task folders in a tmpfs `/work` (8 GB) so that nothing of a user stays once the container stops, `HOME` in `/tmp`; its secrets file, ignored by git, holds `SUPABASE_URL`, `TRAINING_SECRET` and `TRAINING_DEVICE`.
- `scripts/merge-pr.mjs`: the only way a PR is merged. `scripts/deploy-function.mjs`: the only way an Edge Function is deployed. Their rules live in `scripts/merge-gate.ts`, tested by `tests/merge-gate.test.ts`.

## Data rules in detail

- The client deletes no row and changes neither `pages.notebook_id` nor `pages.chapter_id`: a page is moved and a page, a chapter or a notebook is deleted only by `move_page`, `delete_page`, `delete_chapter` and `delete_notebook` (server only, the owner given by the verified token). Each answers an outcome (`moved`, `unchanged`, `deleted`, `page_not_found`, `chapter_not_found`, `notebook_not_found`, `page_processing`; `delete_notebook` also answers the ids of the pages it deleted), refuses a page being read, and takes the advisory lock of the user first (the one `charge_page` and the transfer take), then its row locks notebook first, then chapter, then pages by id (`supabase/tests/concurrency/library-race.sh` plays them all at once). A chapter name is unique in its notebook; deleting a chapter keeps its pages, out of any chapter; deleting a notebook deletes its pages, and the charges of deleted pages stay with no page. The functions touch no file: the Edge Function erases the files first. A transfer merges chapters of the same name like notebooks. Every foreign key `(…, user_id)` has an index starting with its columns.
- Reading A is written only by `record_reading_a` (server only): the reading, its positions in `page_tokens`, `pages.language` and the `missing_line` alert of the page (`p_alerts`: no other kind, one of a kind a page, one already there kept as it is), in one transaction, the page row locked. It answers `recorded`, or refuses with `page_not_found` (no such page, or another owner), `not_text`, `already_read` or `model_not_found` (`p_model_id` names no model of the owner); it keeps the personal model that read A in `readings.htr_model_id` (a foreign key on the model and the owner together, set to null when the model is erased), raises `invalid_model` when `p_model` is `personal` without `p_model_id` or another name with one, and raises `rules_without_model_in_service` when a position carries a `rule` or a `channel` while `private.model_in_service` (the model of A is the owner's `active` model with a `cer` of at most 0.129; nobody may call it but the database) says no; on a refusal or an error the caller erases the ALTO file it uploaded. A reading A has an `alto_path` (a file name directly in its page folder) and none of the settings of a Gemini call, B1 and B2 the reverse; a page has one A at most. A page leaves `text` only once its A and its positions are erased (trigger `private.check_page_type`).
- Corrections and page alerts (slice 4). `page_tokens` also holds `reading_b2`, `review_status` (`pending`, `confirmed`, `corrected`, `skipped`, only for a position of the `queue` channel, which starts `pending`) and the place of the word in the raw text of B1 (`b1_start` included, `b1_end` excluded, in characters; a place never overlaps another and follows the rank, a page has all its places or none; the server writes them through `record_reading_a`, whose token objects may carry `reading_b2`, `b1_start` and `b1_end`). The text a page shows (`page_texts.text`) is rebuilt by `private.recompute_page_text` from the B1 reading, the places and, for each position, its last correction, else the word a rule wrote, else the slice of B1 as it was, so that line breaks and punctuation never change; a page whose positions have no place keeps its text. The client writes `corrections` (insert of `page_id`, `token_index`, `corrected_text`, `chosen_from`, `from_queue` only, no update, no delete): `private.prepare_correction` locks the page, gives the `revision` (1 to 100 a position, 2,000 corrections a page) and `previous_text`, keeps `from_queue` only on a pending position, and refuses a word that is not one word of 1 to 100 characters (no space, control, bidirectional or invisible character from its fixed list), a position that has no place, and a `chosen_from` of `A`, `B1` or `B2` whose word is not exactly that reading of the position; `private.apply_correction` moves a pending queue position to `confirmed` or `corrected` and recalculates the text. Undoing a correction adds one that returns to the previous word. `page_alerts` (`date_incoherent`, `missing_line`, `math_incomplete`, one of each kind a page, `details` an object of 16 KB at most) is written by the server only (`math_incomplete` only by `record_math_alert`, which refuses a page that is not math or lacks B1 or B2, and counts that are negative or both zero). A page has at most one reading B2 (`readings_one_b2_per_page`). `set_page_math` erases the positions (and with them the corrections), the `missing_line` alert and A, and gives the text of B1 back. `supabase/tests/concurrency/corrections-race.sh` plays 20 corrections at once on one page.
- Text of the user (slice 4, `t4/edit`). `page_gaps` (`page_id`, `gap_index`, `text` of 100,000 characters at most; read by its owner, written by the server only) holds the text between the words of a page: gap `n` is what comes before the position of rank `n` (counted from 0), the last gap what comes after the last position, and on a page of math, which has no position, the whole text in gap 0. `private.recompute_page_text` takes the gap in place of the slice of B1 when there is one, and rebuilds a page of math from its gap 0 or from B1. A correction may be empty (a deleted word) only when the server writes it (`auth.uid()` empty): a client's empty correction is refused. `edit_page(page, owner, text, words, gaps)` (server only, owner given by the verified token) is the only way the text of the user is written, and `set_page_math` and `set_page_text` erase it and show B1 again. `supabase/tests/concurrency/edit-race.sh` plays edits and corrections at once on one page (like `corrections-race.sh`).
- Search (slice 7, `t7/search-db`). `public.search_pages(p_query, p_notebook_id default null)` is the one function the client calls to search; it runs with the rights of its owner and an empty search path, and keeps `user_id = auth.uid()` on every table by hand (no account, no query or an empty one: no rows). Words come from the text search configuration `public.search_simple` (the `simple` configuration, accents removed by `unaccent`, case ignored, the whole of a hyphenated word dropped so only its parts count, tags never words), so a query is cut into its distinct words in order. Refused with `22023`: a query over 100 characters (`query_too_long`) or of more than 8 distinct words (`query_too_many_words`); a query with no word finds nothing. A page is found when every word is in its shown text (`page_texts.text`, rank 1) or in the shown text or another reading of it (`reading_a`, `reading_b1`, `reading_b2` of a position, or the `raw_text` of its B1 or B2, rank 2), a page of rank 1 never comes again at rank 2, and a page of math is found at rank 1 only. Only pages whose `uploaded_at` is set and that have a shown text; a notebook given (or not the caller's) narrows the search. At most 50 pages an answer, rank 1 first, the most recent `captured_at` first in each rank. Each row has `page_id`, `notebook_id`, `rank`, `token_index` (the first position that carries the first word of rank 1, or the first word the shown text lacks at rank 2: the shown word of a position is its last correction, else the word a rule wrote; null without such a position), `excerpt` (4,000 characters at most), `match_start` and `match_end` (in characters, `match_end` excluded, of the word in the excerpt, null when none is marked) and `also_read`. Rank 1: the excerpt is the line of the shown text that carries the word, marked. Rank 2 with a position: the line of the shown text at the place of the position (counted in newlines of B1, only when the shown text has as many lines as B1), else the line of B1 at that place or at `line_index`, else the form itself; nothing marked; `also_read` is the form the other reading wrote. Rank 2 without a position: the line of B1 or B2 that carries the word, marked, `also_read` the form as written there. Full-text indexes: `page_texts_search_idx`, `page_tokens_search_idx` (the three readings of a position together) and `readings_search_idx` (B1 and B2 only). Known limits of the parser, kept: text between `<` and `>` is read as a tag and its words are not indexed, and a missing space after a dot (`Mr.Magellan`) makes one `host` or `file` word. `supabase/tests/database/search.test.sql` covers it.
- `pages.page_type` (`text` by default) and `pages.language` (two lowercase letters, or nothing) are written by the server only: by `read-page` and `relaunch-page` with B1, and by `set_page_math` and `set_page_text` (server only) through `set-page-type`. `notebooks.correction_level` stays `course` unless its owner has an `active` personal model in `htr_models` (trigger `private.check_correction_level`), and the client never writes it. `htr_models` holds the generic `medium` (no owner, no file: it is in the image) and, from slice 5, personal models, written by `report_training_task` alone with their `sha256` and `faults_correct` (the planted faults of the test they write in their correct form); `cer` is a fraction (0.129 is 12.9 %).
- Lexicon (slice 7, `t7/lexicon`). `public.lexicon_entries` (`notebook_id`, `user_id`, `form_key`, `form`, `occurrences`, `confirmed`; key `(notebook_id, form_key)`) is read by its owner and written by the server alone, by `private.refresh_lexicon`. A notebook's lexicon is always the result of `private.lexicon_notebook(notebook_id)`, a full recomputation over the pages of the notebook that have a shown text and are not of type `math`: every word of the shown text (tags removed, cut at hyphens and apostrophes, parts of one letter, without a letter, over 40 characters, or ordinal like `1er` left out; a hyphenated or elided word that is whole in the dictionary left out) is a candidate form when it is absent from `private.dictionary_words`, or starts with a capital without being the first word of its line or following `.`, `!`, `?` or `…`, or carries both a letter and a digit, or is followed by a full stop and then a lowercase letter or a digit. A form enters from two pages that carry it, or from one when `confirmed`: its key is among the words of the last correction of a position of the notebook or of a `page_gaps` text. `form_key` is the form in lowercase without accents, `form` the spelling that appears most often (ties: the smallest). At most 5,000 forms a notebook (confirmed first, then the most frequent). Pages read, corrected, edited, moved, switched to or from math, erased, and notebooks erased or merged by a transfer queue the notebook in `private.lexicon_pending` (triggers on `page_texts`, `corrections`, `page_gaps`, `pages`); a deferred constraint trigger on that table recomputes each queued notebook once, when the operation ends, under the advisory lock `lexicon:<notebook id>` taken at that moment, after the page locks of the operation. The recomputation takes a key-share lock on the notebook row (foreign key), so `delete_notebook` and `transfer_account_rows` lock notebooks `for no key update`, and `transfer_account_rows` takes the lexicon locks of the notebooks that move before it touches their lexicon rows. A word of more than 40 characters is never read. The migration queues every notebook that already has a page text, so existing notebooks get their lexicon when it is applied. `transfer_account_rows` moves the lexicon rows of the notebooks that move; a merged notebook is recomputed. `private.dictionary_words` (about 400,000 lowercase words: `wfrench` 1.2.9 and the lowercase entries of `wamerican` 2020.12.07, built by `supabase/dictionary/build.mjs`; licences in `docs/dictionary.md`) is unreachable by the client and written by no function. `public.suggest_words(p_query, p_notebook_id default null)` takes the bounds of `search_pages` (same refusals `22023`), and gives, for each word of the query of at least 4 characters that is in no shown text of the searched pages (those with `uploaded_at`), the form of the lexicon of the caller closest to it by trigram similarity of at least 0.5 (ties: confirmed, then more occurrences, then key), as `(word, suggestion)` where `word` is the word of the query lowercased without accents. `supabase/tests/database/lexicon.test.sql`, `lexicon-transfer.test.sql` and `supabase/tests/concurrency/lexicon-race.sh` cover it.
- Date check (slice 5, `t5/dates`). The `date_incoherent` alerts of `page_alerts` are written and erased by `private.refresh_dates(notebook_id)` alone (`record_reading_a` still refuses them; no client or function writes them), as the difference between the alerts of the notebook's pages and `private.date_alerts_notebook(notebook_id)`, a full recomputation: nothing unless the notebook is at `journal`; then the pages with `status = 'read'`, `page_type = 'text'` and `language = 'fr'` whose shown text (`page_texts.text`) has a pair, read by `private.date_pair(text)`: the header is the first line that is not empty among the first 4,000 characters, cut to 200 characters (`CR`, vertical tab, form feed, `NEL`, line and paragraph separators end a line; a no-break space is a space); its number is the first `jour` or `jours` (case free) followed by ASCII spaces, an optional `.` or `:` and 1 to 3 ASCII digits, the word starting the line or following an ASCII sign that is not a letter, digit or underscore and the number ending the line or preceding one (any character outside ASCII glued to them counts as a letter, so the result never depends on the locale of the database; so a header opened by a sign outside ASCII such as `«`, or after a line break other than `LF`, `CR`, vertical tab, form feed, `NEL` or the two Unicode separators, or made of spaces outside ASCII, gives no pair: a page can lose its pair, never gain one); its date is the first valid `d/m/y` (1 to 2, 1 to 2 and 2 to 4 ASCII digits, separators `/`, `.` or `-` with spaces tolerated, not glued to another digit or separator; three digits for the year, a month outside 1 to 12 or a day that the month does not have make a date invalid and the next match is tried; a year under 100 gets 2000 added). The offset of a page is its date as a day number minus its number. `private.date_flags(offsets integer[])` returns, for each pair, whether it is flagged: the reference of a pair is the other pairs without those that the others flag (a pair is flagged by the rest of the others when it sits more than 2 days from their median), a pair is judged only with at least 3 pairs in its reference (so with at least 4 pairs in the notebook), and it is flagged when it sits more than 2 days from the median of its reference. A notebook of more than 500 pairs is not judged (the function refuses more than 500 offsets and the notebook then has no alert). The alert carries `details = {}`: no value is proposed and nothing is written in the text. The notebook is queued in `private.dates_pending` by triggers on `page_texts` (insert, text, delete), `pages` (`notebook_id`, `page_type`, `language`, `status`, delete) and `notebooks` (`correction_level`); a deferred constraint trigger recomputes each queued notebook once at the end of the operation, under the same advisory lock as the lexicon (`lexicon:<notebook id>`, so that the two recomputations of one notebook never wait for each other in opposite orders). It deletes only the alert rows it can lock (`for update skip locked`, a row already being deleted by a cascade is left to that transaction) and inserts an alert with a key-share lock taken `skip locked` on the page row: a page that another operation holds is left to that operation's own recomputation, which sees the committed state of the notebook. A page that moves is judged by the notebook that receives it, and its old alert goes with the next recomputation of that notebook; a notebook that leaves `journal`, or that the loss of the active model puts back at `course` (`htr_models_reset_levels`), loses every date alert. The app reads the alert with the other alerts of the page and shows "La date de cette page ne suit pas celles du carnet." next to the title of a page of text. `supabase/tests/database/date-alerts.test.sql` and `supabase/tests/concurrency/dates-race.sh` cover it.
- Renderings (slice 9, `t9/schema`). `public.page_renderings` (`page_id` primary key, `user_id`, `text` Markdown of 1 to 60,000 characters, `based_on` the SHA-256 fingerprint of the input text in 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, `model`, `prompt_version`, `created_at`) holds at most one rendering per page, with the composite foreign key `(page_id, user_id)` to `pages` (cascade, deferrable): it goes with its page, with the account, and follows `transfer_account_rows`. The owner reads it (`user_id = auth.uid()`); only the server writes it. A rendering is current when `based_on` equals the fingerprint of the shown text; nothing deletes it when the text changes. `public.render_daily_counts` (`user_id`, `day`, `renders` from 0 to 5, key `(user_id, day)`) is readable and writable by the server alone (RLS on, no policy). `public.charge_render(p_user_id)` (server only, owner's rights, empty search path) takes one rendering of the day, the day being the Paris date: it deletes the user's rows of earlier days, then adds one if the count is under 5 and answers `(true, used)`, else answers `(false, 5)` without counting; a null user raises `account_not_found`, an unknown user violates the foreign key (`23503`). The caller calls it before the model, so discarded renderings count; it touches neither `page_charges` nor the monthly limit. `transfer_account_rows` moves the renderings and adds the counts of the giving account to those of the registered one per day (capped at 5). `supabase/tests/database/page-renderings.test.sql` and `page-renderings-transfer.test.sql` cover it, `supabase/tests/concurrency/charge-render.sh` the count under 40 simultaneous calls.
- Age and consents (slice 5, `t5/consent`). `profiles.birth_year` (1900 to the current year in Paris) and `profiles.country` (two capital letters, or empty when the phone has no region) are written once, together, by the client, and only by a registered account (not anonymous, address confirmed); trigger `private.check_profile_age` refuses a profile created with them (`age_set_on_insert`), a second write from anyone (`age_already_set`), `account_not_registered` and `birth_year_out_of_range`. The retained age is the current year in Paris minus the birth year minus one, the youngest possible; the age of digital consent comes from `private.digital_consent_ages` (28 countries, unreachable by the client), 16 for any other country or none (`private.under_consent_age`). `public.consents` (`user_id`, `kind` `training`, `version`, `granted_by` `user` or `parent`, `parent_email` only for `parent`, 254 characters at most, `granted_at`, `revoked_at`) is read by its owner; the client inserts only `kind` and `version`, so only a `user` consent, and never updates or deletes. Trigger `private.check_consent`, for every writer and under the user's advisory lock, refuses `consent_revoked_on_insert`, `account_not_registered`, `consent_version_outdated` (the current version is `private.consent_version`: `training-1`), `age_missing`, `under_age` (a `user` consent below the age), `not_under_age` (a `parent` consent at or above it) and `consent_already_active` (23505; one active consent per user and kind, also held by a unique index), and dates the row itself. Trigger `private.keep_consent` lets an update only set `revoked_at` once, from nothing, and dates it itself (the date the writer gives is replaced by the time of the update); everything else is `consent_immutable`. The `parent` row is written by the server alone (slice 5, `t5/parent`). Deleting the account deletes its consents; an anonymous account never has a birth year or a consent, so the transfer has none to move.
- Parent consent (slice 5, `t5/parent`). `request-parent-consent` takes the account from the verified token alone and a body of exactly `parent_email` (otherwise 400 `invalid_request`); an address outside the allow-list `PARENT_EMAIL` (a local part of at most 64 characters made of letters, digits and `_+-` in dot-separated runs; a domain of labels of letters, digits and inner hyphens, ending with a TLD of letters; no `%`, quote, space or line break) or over 254 characters is 400 `invalid_email` before the database; without `RESEND_API_KEY` it answers 503 `unavailable` and writes nothing. Then `public.request_parent_consent(user, address)` (service only, under the user's advisory lock) refuses, in this order, `account_not_registered` (403), `age_missing` (409), `not_under_age` (409), `consent_already_active` (409), `invalid_email` (400, same allow-list, `private.is_parent_email`), `same_email` (400, the account's own address whatever its case) and `send_limit` (429: three sends of the account in the last 24 hours, counted in `private.parent_consent_sends`; or, under one global advisory lock, three sends to the same address in 7 days across all accounts, or 50 sends in 24 hours across the service, counted in `private.parent_address_sends` by the peppered `public.email_hash` of the address, never the address), else replaces the request in progress with a new one in `private.parent_consent_requests` (one per user: address in lower case, a six-digit code drawn by `gen_random_bytes` and kept as a bcrypt hash, 0 to 5 attempts, valid 7 days), counts a send for the account and for the address and returns the code to the function alone. The function sends the fixed text of `parentEmail` (French then English, the code, no link, no user text) through Resend in plain text, from `onboarding@resend.dev` until the pre-beta domain, with a 10-second timeout; if the send fails it withdraws the request (`withdraw_parent_request`, by its id; the send stays counted) and answers 502 `send_failed`, else 200 `{requested: true}`. `confirm-parent-consent` takes the token and a body of exactly a six-digit `code` (otherwise 400 `invalid_request`); `public.confirm_parent_consent` (service only, under the lock) answers `no_request` (404), `invalid_code` (400), `code_expired` (410, the request removed), `wrong_code` (400 with `attempts_left`, the attempt counted; the fifth removes the request, so even the right code then finds `no_request`) or inserts the `parent` consent with the address of the request through `private.check_consent`, whose refusals it passes on (`not_under_age` and the others, 409 or 403) after removing a request that can no longer succeed, and removes the request: 200 `{confirmed: true}`. The three tables live in `private`, with RLS on and no right to anyone; the requests and sends of an account go with it. The cron job `purge-parent-requests` (02:50 every night, `private.purge_parent_requests`) removes expired requests, account sends older than 24 hours and address sends older than 7 days, so an unanswered parent's address is kept 7 days at most. The logs carry neither the address, the code nor the provider's answer, only `parent_consent_requested`, `parent_consent_confirmed`, `parent_email_failed` (error name and HTTP status) and the refusals. The client cannot see a request in progress: the confirmation answers `no_request`. Withdrawal of the consent (slice 5, `t5/consent-screens`). `revoke-training` takes the account from the verified token alone (an anonymous account too: it simply has nothing to withdraw), ignores the body, and in this order: erases every file of the account in `models` and the folder `{user_id}/writing-tests/` of `photos` (listed, whatever the files are named; a failure stops the call with 500 and nothing is withdrawn, the call can be played again), calls `public.revoke_training(user)` (service only, `security definer`, under the user's advisory lock, in one transaction), then erases the same files once more (a test photo or model that landed meanwhile; a failure is logged `training_files_left`, the withdrawal stands). `revoke_training` dates `revoked_at` of the active `training` consent (the trigger `keep_consent` dates it itself; the row stays as history), deletes the request to a parent in progress (`private.parent_consent_requests`), deletes the personal models of the user (`htr_models` with this `user_id`; the generic model has none) and sets back to `course` every notebook of the user above it, and answers whether a consent was active. Later branches of slice 5 extend it with the writing tests and the training tasks, and refuse a training report that arrives after it. Answers: 200 `{revoked: true}` (also when there was no consent), 401, 405, 500; the log carries `training_revoked` and nothing else, no identifier. The user can consent again afterwards (a new row).

- Writing tests (slice 5, `t5/writing-test`). `public.writing_tests` (`id`, `user_id`, `template` `fr` or `en`, `status` `pending`, `accepted`, `rejected`, `failed` or `replaced`, `training_lines` 0 to 30 and `validation_lines` 0 to 10 once checked, `redo` the sheets to write again, `created_at`, `checked_at`) is read by its owner and written by the server alone through three functions (service only, owner's rights, empty search path, each taking the user's advisory lock first). `begin_writing_test(user, template)` answers `consent_required` without an active training consent, `test_in_progress` (one `pending` test at a time, held by a unique index; a test pending for more than 30 minutes is marked `failed` and its id is returned in `expired` so that the function erases its photos), `test_limit` (three tests created in 24 hours, rejected or not) or `started` with the new id; it forgets tests that are neither pending nor accepted after two days, and an invalid template violates the check (`23514`). `finish_writing_test(user, test, sheet1_training, sheet2_training, sheet2_validation)` (counts 0 to 20, 0 to 10 and 0 to 10, else `invalid_counts`) finishes only a `pending` test of that user with the consent still active (`test_not_found` otherwise, `consent_required` and `failed` if the consent is gone): the test is `accepted` with at least 24 training lines and 8 validation lines, else `rejected` with `redo` naming sheet 1 when it kept fewer than 16 lines and the total is under 24, and sheet 2 when it kept fewer than 8 validation lines or (fewer than 8 training lines and the total is under 24); an accepted test marks the earlier accepted one `replaced` (one accepted test at most, held by a unique index) and returns its id in `replaced`. `fail_writing_test(user, test)` marks a pending test `failed`. `revoke_training` deletes the tests of the user. The photos are `photos/{user_id}/writing-tests/{test_id}/1.jpg` and `2.jpg`, uploaded through signed addresses, read by their owner and erased by the functions below, by `revoke-training` and by `delete-account`. `prepare-writing-test` takes the owner from the verified token alone and a body of exactly `template` (`fr` or `en`, else 400 `invalid_request`), refuses 403 `consent_required`, 409 `test_in_progress` and 429 `test_limit`, erases the files of the tests that expired, and answers `{test_id, uploads: [{path, token}, {path, token}]}`; if the addresses cannot be made the test is marked `failed`. `check-writing-test` takes the owner from the token alone and a body of exactly `test_id`, answers 404 `test_not_found` for anything but a pending test of the caller and 503 `unavailable` without the reading service, then 202 `{checking: true}` and, in the background and once per test in an isolate, reads sheet 1 then sheet 2 through `/check-writing` (a sheet that is missing, over 10 MB, not a JPEG or refused by the service for its photo or its complexity keeps no line; any other failure of the service marks the test `failed`), calls `finish_writing_test` and erases the photos of a test that is not accepted, of the tests an accepted test replaces, and of a test whose consent was withdrawn meanwhile. Logs carry the outcome and the two counts only. `supabase/tests/database/writing-tests.test.sql` covers the functions and `supabase/tests/concurrency/writing-test-race.sh` 40 simultaneous starts, 40 simultaneous finishes and 40 finishes racing a withdrawal.
- Training (slice 5, `t5/trainer`). `public.training_tasks` (`user_id`, `status` `queued`, `claimed`, `done` or `failed`, `attempts` 0 to 2, `attempt_id` the id of the current claim and of the model file it may upload, `base_model_id` the active model at claim time, `claimed_at`, `created_at`, `finished_at`) has no right for the client and one open task (queued or claimed) per user, held by a unique index. A task is due (`private.training_due`) for a user with an active training consent and an accepted writing test, without an open task, when it never had a task, or when its last task is more than 24 hours old and either a test was accepted since or 20 text pages were read (`read_at`) since it; `finish_writing_test` queues it in the same transaction when a test is accepted, and `claim_training_task` queues the due tasks of every user first. Every function is service only, owner's rights, empty search path. `claim_training_task()` takes a lock of its own first, so claims run one at a time; under each user's advisory lock (skipped while busy) it puts back in the queue a task claimed more than 2 hours ago (once: the second time it fails); it answers nothing while a task is claimed, so one task is trained at a time for the whole service; then it queues the due tasks (user's advisory lock, skipped while busy), picks the oldest queued task, takes its user's advisory lock before locking the row (the order of every other function), fails it without consent or accepted test, gives it a new `attempt_id` and answers it as JSON: `task_id` (the attempt), `user_id`, `test_id`, `template`, the active personal `model` (`path`, `sha256`) or null, and the 300 most recent read text pages of the user (`page_type` `text`, `status` `read`, none with `page_gaps`, that is retouched by "Modifier"), each with its B1 (`readings`), its `language` and, per corrected position, the last correction and the `reading_a` of `page_tokens`. `training_upload_target(attempt)` answers `ready` with the path `{user_id}/{attempt}.safetensors`, or `task_not_found`, `task_expired`, `consent_required`. `report_training_task(attempt, outcome, cer, base_cer, faults, base_faults, alphabet, samples, sha256)` (bounds: CER 0 to 10, a fraction, faults 0 to 8, alphabet 1 to 10,000, samples 1 to 20,000, a lowercase SHA-256, else `invalid_report` `22023`) takes the user's lock and answers an outcome and the model files to erase: `task_not_found` (no such claimed attempt, also after a withdrawal: the file of that attempt is named for erasure), `task_expired`, `consent_required`, `failed`, `model_missing` (no uploaded file, or above 100 MB), `rejected` (the active model changed since the claim, or the new CER is not below the base CER, or it writes more faults correctly than the base: its file is erased and no row is written) or `activated` (the new model becomes `active`, the replaced one `retired` and keeps its file, any older personal model is deleted with its file). `revoke_training` deletes the tasks. The `models` bucket takes `application/octet-stream` up to 100 MB. The three functions `training-claim`, `training-upload` and `training-report` have no user token (`verify_jwt = false`): each refuses everything unless `TRAINING_SECRET` is set to 32 characters at least and sent in `X-Training-Secret` (constant time), and takes a JSON object of exactly its fields: `{}`; `{task_id}`; `{task_id, outcome: "failed"}` or `{task_id, outcome: "trained", cer, base_cer, faults, base_faults, alphabet, samples, sha256}` (400 `invalid_request` otherwise). `training-claim` answers `{task: null}` or `{task: {task_id, template, test, model, pages}}`, every address signed for one hour and given without its host (the trainer prefixes its own `SUPABASE_URL`): the two photos of the accepted test (if one is missing, or the active model, the task is failed and `{task: null}` answered), the active model with its `sha256`, and per page `photo`, `b1`, `language`, `corrections` (`token_index`, `text`, `reading_a`); a page whose photo is gone is left out; no user, page or test id leaves. `training-upload` answers `{url}`, an upload address signed for that one path (upsert), or 404 `task_not_found`, 409 `task_expired` or `consent_required`. `training-report` erases the files the database names, then answers `{outcome}` or 404 `task_not_found`, 409 `task_expired` or `consent_required`. Logs carry outcomes and counts only. `supabase/tests/database/training-tasks.test.sql` covers the rules and `supabase/tests/concurrency/training-race.sh` 40 simultaneous claims and 40 reports racing their withdrawal.
- Correction levels (slice 5, `t5/levels`). `notebooks.correction_level` (`course`, `journal`, `grammar`) is written by the server only. `private.has_model_in_service(user)` is true when the user has an active model that `private.model_in_service` accepts (CER of 0.129 at most): the trigger `check_correction_level` refuses a level above `course` otherwise (`correction_level_requires_personal_model`, 23514), on insert and on update of the level or of the owner. The only path for the app is `set-correction-level` (verified token, a body of exactly `notebook_id` and `level`, otherwise 400 `invalid_request`), which calls `set_correction_level(user, notebook, level)` (server only): it takes the user's advisory lock, then answers `notebook_not_found` (404; the notebook of another account is not found either), `requires_model` (409; only for `journal` and `grammar`) or `set` (200 `{level}`), and raises `invalid_level` for a level outside the three; it logs the level and no identifier. An active model that is not in service (a replacement above 12.9 %, or a measure that rises) puts every notebook of its owner back at `course` through the trigger `htr_models_reset_levels`; retiring the old model alone does not. The withdrawal (`revoke_training`) takes the same lock and puts every notebook back at `course`, so a change and a withdrawal in parallel end at `course`. `training_stage()` (signed-in users only, no argument, `auth.uid()` alone) answers the stage of the account's reading of its handwriting: `not_started`, `parent_pending` (a request to a parent not expired and no consent), `test_needed` (consent, no active model, no accepted test), `training` (accepted test, no active model), `in_service` or `not_precise` (an active model with a CER above 0.129 or none). The app asks it fresh each time (`trainingStageQuery`); the training screen shows it under the state, and the menu of a notebook (`useLevelItem`) reads the level and the stage when opened, lists the three levels (the current one marked), writes the course level at once, and a higher level only when the stage is `in_service`; otherwise it answers with the sentence of the stage and writes nothing.
