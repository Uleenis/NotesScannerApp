# NotesScannerApp

iOS app that turns photos of handwritten notes into faithful digital text. It never invents text: fidelity to the page comes before readability.

## Stack

- App: Expo SDK 57, React Native 0.86, React 19, TypeScript strict. Expo has changed: read the versioned docs at https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/ before writing app code.
- Backend: Supabase (Postgres, Auth, Storage, Edge Functions in TypeScript on Deno), Paris region.
- Reading model: `gemini-3.6-flash`, prompt V0, default temperature, `thinkingBudget: 0`, called only from Edge Functions.
- Reading service: Python 3.13 in `services/reading/` (FastAPI, Kraken 7.1.1, PyTorch for the processor only), one Docker image. It runs on the PC next to the local Supabase stack until Cloud Run, in the pre-beta slice (after slice 8, with the paid accounts).

## Layout

- `src/app/`: routes (Expo Router) and their `_layout.tsx`, nothing else.
- `src/components/`: shared components.
- `src/i18n/`: language files (`en.ts`, `fr.ts`) and the choice of language.
- `src/auth/`: the anonymous account, the provider that opens it when the app starts and follows every change of account (sign-up, sign-in, sign-out), and the actions of the account by e-mail code (`account.ts`, `account-errors.ts`, `account-service.ts`). A sign-in from an anonymous account that has pages checks its code on a second client that keeps nothing (`getStatelessSupabase`), calls `transfer-account` with both tokens, and only then gives the account found to the client of the phone.
- `src/lib/`: the Supabase client, its config, the secure session storage, the addresses of the terms and the privacy policy (`legal-links.ts`), the opening of a web address (`open-link.ts`) and the forgetting of the data of other accounts.
- `src/notebooks/`: reading, creating and caching notebooks.
- `src/capture/`: the limits shown before the camera and the hook that opens the camera or the limit sheet, sending a page (create, prepare, upload, mark it uploaded, start the reading), preparing the photo, the camera and network hooks. The photo is saved again in the sense of the viewfinder, never in the sense the phone guessed from gravity: the camera gives its orientation tag (`exif: true`), the app turns the image back to the viewfinder (plus the quarter turns of "Rotate"), shrinks it above 12 Mpx, and saves one JPEG with no metadata. The viewfinder and the check of the photo have the shape of the photo (3:4). A page only counts for the user (notebook counts, last notebook, page lists) once its `uploaded_at` is set, so a page created for a photo that never arrived stays invisible.
- `src/pages/`: the pages a user sees (list, one page with its number and text, signed address of its photo, starting or restarting a reading), what a page shows according to its status, the title of a page and the gluing of its cut lines at display time (the text itself is never changed), dates without `Intl`, and the polling delays. Every query here keeps to pages whose `uploaded_at` is set. `formulas.ts` cuts the body of a page of math into text and formulas (`$…$` on one line with no space inside its dollars, `$$…$$`, `\(…\)`, `\[…\]`, `\begin{…}…\end{…}`; anything unclosed stays text) and into rows; `tex.ts` draws one formula as an SVG string with MathJax 4 (`@mathjax/src` and its font, Apache-2.0) on the phone, with the TeX packages `base` and `ams` only (no `\href`, `\url`, `\class`, `\style`, `\require`), every font range it may need imported with the app so nothing is ever fetched, and an output with a link or an image refused; it refuses before drawing a formula over 2,000 characters, nested deeper than 20 (counted with what is open: a brace, a `\left` until its `\right`, an environment until its `\end`), using `\DeclareMathOperator` or `\pmb` (their nesting doubles the work), `\mmlToken` (MathML attributes), `\newcolumntype`, `\label`, `\tag`, `\ref`, `\eqref`, `\hsize`, `\vbox`, `\vtop`, `\vcenter` or `\parbox` (the last five start a slow line breaking), an environment outside the common ones (the matrices, `cases`, `aligned`, `align`, `gathered`, `gather`, `equation`, `split`, `multline`, `flalign`, `eqnarray`, `array`; so no `alignat` nor `subarray`), an `array` whose column template is not 1 to 20 columns `l`, `c` or `r` with at most one `|` around each, a `\begin` whose name cannot be read, or a `%` comment (it would hide what it closes from the count); after drawing it refuses an SVG over 512 KB, larger than 300 by 120 ex or 4,000 square ex, of more than 3,000 elements, or holding a link, an image or a `url(`. Each formula gets a fresh TeX input and a fresh SVG output, so nothing it defines or sets reaches the next; the drawing must be one root picture (inline line breaks are off). Formulas are drawn one at a time, each after a turn of the screen, a formula asked twice waits for the first drawing, a drawing still queued when its formula leaves the screen is dropped (`releaseTex`), a page draws at most 100 formulas and shows the rest as read, and the last 50 results are kept (the oldest goes first) and read at once. A formula it refuses is shown as it was read, never as an error. `words.ts` reads the positions (`page_tokens`) and corrections of a page by pages of 1,000 rows (20 reads at most, else the page is not editable) and places each word in the text the page shows: from the places in B1 (`b1_start`, `b1_end`, in characters) shifted by the length of every word a correction or a rule changed, checked against the text (a changed word that is not where it should be, a word without a place, overlapping places or a place past the end make the whole page not editable, never a guess); the suggestions of a word are its distinct readings of A, B1 and B2, in that order. `isWord` is the client's copy of the word rule of `corrections` (1 to 100 characters, none of the fixed list of spaces, controls and invisible characters), and `addCorrection` sends only `page_id`, `token_index`, `corrected_text`, `chosen_from` and `from_queue: false`, and nothing when the word fails `isWord`. `displayPieces` (`display.ts`) gives the same title and blocks as `displayDocument` as pieces that each know their place in the text, so a word can be found in the glued lines. The engine itself (`mathjax-engine.ts`) is loaded on the first formula, not at app start. On a page of math the first line is the title only when no formula starts on it; display formulas scroll sideways when wider than the screen, and an inline formula wider than 36 ex is shown as read. `mathjax-context.ts`, imported first, gives React Native's `navigator` the `appVersion` and `userAgent` MathJax reads at load. `metro.config.js` resolves the package subpath imports of MathJax (`#default-font/*` and kin), whose bare targets Metro 0.84 cannot follow. `edit.ts` is the "Modifier" mode: `alignEdit` takes the text shown, the placed words and the text the user wrote and answers `same`, `refused` (a text over 100,000 characters, more than 1,000 differences between the two lists of words, or more than 2,000 words changed) or the words and gaps to send; it matches the words of the two texts with the word rule of the service (the same regular expression, combining marks counted as letters) by a Myers difference, exact word for exact word (a position whose word holds a character outside that rule, kept by an earlier correction, is looked for whole in the new text first), pairs in order the words that changed inside a hole of the difference (a position left without a word is deleted, a word left without a position stays in the text between the words), keeps every other character (added words, line cuts, punctuation, spaces) as the text of the user in the gaps, and checks that the words and gaps give the text exactly back; `editPage` calls `edit-page`. `words.ts` reads `page_gaps` too (`gaps` in `PageWords`) and `placeWords` places each word after the gap that comes before it, a deleted word having no length (never tappable). `alerts.ts` reads the kinds of alert a page shows (`missing_line` on a page of text, `math_incomplete` on a page of math). `use-text-edit.ts` holds the state of the edit: the draft, the problem (`tooBig`, `changed`, `refused`, `failed`) and the save.
- `src/components/account/`: the groups and rows of the account screen.
- `src/components/reading/`: the screens' parts for a reading: ink stroke, waiting view, document, original photo with its zoom, page rows and cards. Under the title of a read document, a page of text or of math shows the "math page" switch, which calls `set-page-type` and comes back to the stored type when the call fails; a page in an unsupported script shows instead a sentence saying the reading AI alone reads it. The body of a page of math is drawn with its formulas (`formula.tsx`, through `react-native-svg`, labelled with its LaTeX for screen readers), the rest of the text in Source Serif 4; a page of text keeps its LaTeX as it was read. On a page of text that is read, every word that `placeWords` can place is tappable (`piece-text.tsx`): a tap marks it and opens `word-editor.tsx` above the keyboard (a field, Cancel, OK, and the readings of A, B1 and B2 as suggestions); a suggestion is saved with its source as `chosen_from`, a typed word as `keyboard`, a word left as it is is not saved, and a refused or failed save keeps the field open with a sentence. Switching a page to math asks first when the page has corrections, or when the app cannot tell. A read page of text whose words are all placed, or of math, shows "Edit" (`page-text-edit.tsx`): the text becomes one field with Cancel and Save above the keyboard, the sentence of a problem under them; the alerts of the page are one neutral sentence each under the title. Switching a page to math or back asks first when the page holds corrections or text of the user (words read with the page, for a page of math too).
- `src/theme.ts`: colors, fonts and type sizes of the design direction.
- `tests/`: Jest tests. `tests/fixtures/b1-line-shapes.json` holds only the shape of the lines of the 18 B1 readings of the Thinking Spike (every letter is `x` or `X`, every digit `0`), with the verdict of the ground truth for each line break; no text of a page may enter the repository. `tests/google-keys.test.ts` reads every file git would send (tracked, or new and not ignored) and fails on a Google key or identifier, naming the file and the line, never the value.
- `supabase/migrations/`: the only way the schema changes.
- `supabase/templates/`: the e-mail templates (sign-up, sign-in, confirmation). Each one shows `{{ .Token }}`, the code, and never a link; `config.toml` references them and sets the code at 6 digits, valid 10 minutes, a new one after 30 seconds, and the proof of the address before an account keeps it.
- `supabase/functions/`: Edge Functions (`prepare-page`, `read-page`, `relaunch-page`, `sweep-alto`, `set-page-type`, `delete-account`, `transfer-account`, `delete-page`, `delete-notebook`, `delete-chapter`, `move-page`, `edit-page`), their shared code in `_shared/`, their Deno tests in `tests/`. The last four take the owner from the verified token alone, and a body of exactly their ids (`page_id`; `notebook_id`; `chapter_id`; `page_id`, `notebook_id` and `chapter_id`, null for no chapter), nothing else. `delete-page` and `delete-notebook` read the state first (not found: 404; a page `processing`: 409 `page_processing`, before any file is touched), erase the folder `{user_id}/{page_id}/` of every page in `photos` and `alto` by listing it (whatever the files are named), call the SQL function (`delete_page`, `delete_notebook`), which rechecks under lock, then erase `photos` and `alto` once more, for the pages listed and for the ids `delete_notebook` answers (an A that ended or a photo that landed meanwhile, a page that came into the notebook after the list; a failure there is logged `files_left`, the deletion stands). `delete-chapter` and `move-page` touch no file. Answers: 200 `{deleted: true}` or `{moved: true|false}`, 400 `invalid_request`, 401, 404 `page_not_found`, `notebook_not_found` or `chapter_not_found`, 409 `page_processing`; logs carry a page id at most. `set-page-type` switches a read page of its caller (the owner from the verified token alone) between `text` and `math`; the body holds exactly `page_id` and `page_type` (`text` or `math`, never `unsupported_script`). To math, `set_page_math` (server only, the page row locked) erases A, its positions and the language and answers the ALTO path, which the function erases (a failure is logged `alto_left`, the switch stands). To text, `set_page_text` sets the type and answers B1 (its one `B1` reading), and A is read again in the background from the stored photo, as in `read-page`: no charge, no Gemini. The stored B2 of the page (its one `B2` reading, read beside the function with the service key) goes to `/arbitrate` with B1. A is read again at most once in ten minutes and three times in all for a page (`pages.a_relaunches`, `pages.a_relaunched_at`, written under the same lock, never by the client); past that the type still switches and no A starts (`a_abandoned relaunch_limit`); without a reading service configured, the switch counts nothing (`a_abandoned no_service`). A photo that is missing, not a JPEG or cannot be downloaded leaves the page text without A, and the switch is answered 200. A page not read, in an unsupported script or of another owner is refused; a switch to the type the page has changes nothing. An A that ends after a switch to math is refused by `record_reading_a` (`not_text`). `read-page` and `relaunch-page` share one reading (`_shared/reading.ts`): once the page is claimed, its photo goes at the same time to Gemini (B1), to Gemini again (B2, `_shared/reading-b2.ts`: the same call on its own, so neither reading receives the other nor A, and it is never charged) and to `/recognize` of the reading service (A, `_shared/reading-a.ts`). B1 decides the type of the page (`_shared/page-type.ts`: `math` when it holds a LaTeX delimiter or math command, `$…$` only on one line and when the opening `$` is followed and the closing `$` preceded by a character that is not a space, `$$…$$` when it holds a character that is not a space, `unsupported_script` when most of its letters are not Latin, else `text`), written with `read` in one update; the answer goes back without waiting for A or B2, which go on in the background (`EdgeRuntime.waitUntil`). A B2 that fails is tried once more, then the page stays without one; it is written to `readings` (`label = B2`) only once B1 is stored, on a page of text or of math (cancelled on `unsupported_script`, and dropped when B1 fails, its retry skipped), also when it comes after the answer. Once B2 is written, and when `set-page-type` switches a page to math, `_shared/math-check.ts` checks the page of math for a missing statement (`checkMathPage`, in the background, never an error): it reads B1 and B2 back through `math_check_readings` (server only, empty unless the page is math and has one B1 and one B2), cuts each into statements and normalizes them as `Math Spike/scripts/units.py` does (statement closed by a relation followed by a balanced operand, LaTeX, superscripts, dashes, brackets and spaces unified, plus the exponent or index in braces of one to three characters read as without, and a statement with no letter, digit or empty set left out), compares the two as multisets with no position, and, when one pass has a statement the other lacks, calls `record_math_alert` (server only, the page row locked, math only) with two counts, `only_b1` and `only_b2`, no text. It compares nothing above 60,000 characters a pass or past a work budget of 3,000,000 normalized characters (logged `math_checked` with `skipped`, never an error); the log carries `complete`, `incomplete` or `skipped` and the page id, never a statement. `set_page_text` erases the `math_incomplete` alert. `/arbitrate` gets `b2` when B2 was written, waited for until the budget of A minus the arbitrate timeout (30 s) is spent (`b2_late`), else without. On a text page A and B1 go to `/arbitrate`; on any other type, or when B1 fails, A is cancelled and nothing is written. A failure that may pass (network, timeout, 429, 5xx) is tried once more, a refusal never; then the page stays with B1 alone. `_shared/reading-service.ts` is the only code that calls the service: the secret in `X-Reading-Secret`, no redirect, https, or http only when `SUPABASE_URL` is itself http (the local stack), to `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `::1` or a name without a dot, and with no proxy of the environment on the way; the budget of A is 140 s from the send, a timeout of `/recognize` or of `/arbitrate` is never tried again (the call goes on in the service); the answer of `/recognize` passed on as it came. Without `READING_URL` and `READING_SECRET` the page is read by B1 alone; with one of them only, a secret under 32 characters or with spaces around it, an address that is not an origin alone or not https (outside the local stack), or a proxy that would see the secret, the page is read by B1 alone too and the start logs `reading_service_misconfigured` with a fixed code, never a value. `sweep-alto` is called each night at 02:30 UTC by the `pg_cron` task `sweep-alto` (`private.sweep_alto`, its secret and address `sweep_secret` and `sweep_url` in Vault, never the relaunch ones; the address must be https, or http to the local stack, and end in `/functions/v1/sweep-alto`), with its own secret in `X-Sweep-Secret` (`SWEEP_SECRET`, 32 characters at least, no space around it, compared in constant time) and a body of exactly `{}`; it erases from `alto` alone the files `alto_orphans` (server only) names: in `alto`, uploaded and last written more than 24 hours ago, shaped `{uuid}/{uuid}/{file}`, and named by no reading, the user folder left aside (a transfer renames the reading before it copies the file), 1,000 names a call at most; it goes by batches of 500, 20 at most and stops after a minute, or when the storage removed fewer than asked; it never touches `photos`. Answers: 200 `{removed: n}`, 400 `invalid_request`, 401, 405; it logs a count and a duration only, also when a batch fails after others. Local secrets (`GEMINI_API_KEY`, `RELAUNCH_SECRET`, `SWEEP_SECRET`, `READING_URL`, `READING_SECRET`) live in `supabase/functions/.env`, ignored by git. The `pg_cron` tasks that call `relaunch-page` and `sweep-alto` read their secret and address from Supabase Vault, never from the repository. The `pg_cron` task `purge-cron-history` (`private.purge_cron_history`, executable by nobody, every night at 02:45 UTC) erases the runs of `cron.job_run_details` that ended more than 30 days ago, and keeps a run still going.
- `edit-page` (same folder) takes the owner from the verified token alone and a body of exactly `page_id`, `text`, `words` (at most 2,000 of exactly `token_index` and `text`, at most 100 characters, empty to delete a word, no index twice) and `gaps` (at most 20,001 of exactly `gap_index` and `text`, no index twice, their texts together no longer than `text`); `text` is at most 200,000 characters and the body 2,000,000 (checked on `content-length` first); no text, word or gap holds a NUL or a lone surrogate. It only passes them to `edit_page` (server only), which takes the lock of the page, checks them again, inserts each word as a correction from the keyboard, writes the gaps (and drops those equal to what B1 had), rebuilds the text once (the trigger of corrections is told to wait by a transaction-local setting only `edit_page` sets) and refuses with `text_mismatch`, undoing everything, unless the text is exactly `text`. Answers: 200 `{edited: true}`, 400 `invalid_request`, 401, 404 `page_not_found`, 409 `page_not_read`, `unsupported_script`, `not_editable` (a page of text with no place for its words) or `text_changed`, 422 `invalid_edit`; logs carry a page id at most.
- `supabase/tests/database/`: pgTAP tests.
- `supabase/tests/concurrency/`: parallel-call tests.
- `services/reading/`: the reading service. `src/reading/segmentation.py` cuts a photo into lines with the chain of the Frame Spike, every setting unchanged: Kraken's `blla` finds baseline fragments, fragments of the same height make one row, `ink.py` keeps the ink components under a threshold set from the page's own ink (so a printed grid goes and a light pencil stroke stays), each component goes to its row (a component across two rows is cut), and each row becomes one straightened grey tile at most 1,700 px wide, its neighbours' ink erased, rows in order from the top. Each line keeps its ink box on the photo and the placement that carries a point of the tile back to the photo (continuous coordinates: the centre of pixel `(u, v)` is `(u + 0.5, v + 0.5)`). `blla` runs on the processor and on one thread, as `kraken segment` does by default, so the lines of a photo do not depend on the number of cores; the pinned tiles of the spikes came from `blla` on a graphics card, so 77 of their 310 tiles differ slightly from the service's (measure in `04 Testing/T2 mesures/segmentation/`, outside the repository). The thread limit is process-wide: keep segmentation off threads that run other work. `src/reading/recognition.py` reads each tile as one whole line with `medium` on the processor, one thread, as `kraken ocr --no-segmentation` did for the Cold Spike; a word is a run between spaces, its box runs from the cut of its first character to the cut of its last (one tile pixel wide for a one-letter word), carried onto the photo, and its confidence is the mean of its characters rounded to 4 decimals, as Kraken's ALTO writes it. `Reader` reads one page at a time. `POST /recognize?page_id=<lowercase uuid>` takes the photo as the raw body with exactly one `Content-Type: image/jpeg`, and nothing else (any other query field or body type is refused), and answers `page_id`, `width`, `height`, `lines` (`row`, `box` of the tile on the photo, `ink` box, `words` with `text`, `box`, `confidence`) and `alto`; it writes nothing. Refusals have fixed bodies: 400 `invalid_request` or `invalid_photo` (not baseline or progressive, more than 16 scans, broken), 413 `too_large` (above 10 MB, 12 Mpx or 8,000 px a side), 415 `unsupported_media_type`, 422 `too_complex` (more than 40,000 ink components, or more than 120 s of reading), 503 `busy` (four requests already in flight), 500 `reading_failed`. `POST /arbitrate` takes exactly one `Content-Type: application/json` and a body of exactly `page_id`, `user_id` (lowercase uuids), `b1` (the text B1 shows), `b2` (optional, the text of B2, bounded as `b1`), `a` (the answer of `/recognize` as it came, its ALTO checked against the ALTO its lines give) and `duration_ms`; it needs no Kraken work and does not wait for a reading. `src/reading/tokens.py` cuts a text into words as `Disagreement Spike/scripts/tokens.py` does (same matches, same words left out), but keeps each word as written in B1 (case, apostrophes, hyphens) and compares a form where apostrophes and dashes are unified, case kept. `src/reading/alignment.py` is the block aligner of `Signal Spike/scripts/align_blocks.py` (`align_free`), unchanged: B1 is the pivot, a line `/recognize` cut but read empty is a declared hole, a hole costs nothing and absorbs at most the surplus of words of B1 over A (and the length of as many of B1's longest lines); a page above 1,000,000 cells of the table, or whose letters of B1 times letters of A pass 50,000,000, answers 422 `too_complex`. `src/reading/arbitration.py` gives one position per word of B1: its rank, its line in B1, the word of A paired with it, and its anchor: the box of that word of A; else the `box` of the line of A nearest on the same line of B1; else, in a hole, the line of the hole (one line of B1 per line of the hole, or the box spanning the hole when their numbers differ); else none. The gate is open only on an `active` personal model whose `cer` is at most 0.129; closed, no rule runs and `rule` and `channel` stay empty. `src/reading/rules.py` holds the rules, gate open, in the order 2, 5, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 of the Arbitrage (rule 1 waits for slice 5): the type is the strictest of A, B1 and B2 (a digit in any makes a number; a proper noun flagged by the entities port on B1, on the word of A paired with it or on the word of B2 paired with it); readings are compared in their form (case kept, apostrophes and dashes unified); rule 2 (number read differently) writes B1 to `queue`; rule 5 (B1 and B2 differ and A confirms one of them) writes the word A confirms, as that pass read it, to `layer`; rule 3 (proper noun) B1 to `layer`; rule 4 (common word, A at a confidence of at least 0.990, one edit, homophones by the phonemes port, not an accent alone, not a truncation) A on a `journal` notebook, else B1, to `layer`; rule 6 (B1 and B2 differ and A confirms neither, or read nothing there) B1 to `queue`; rule 7 (the word is on a line B2 lacks) B1 to `none`, the alert being the page's; rule 8 (any other disagreement) B1 to `layer`; rule 9 (A and B1 agree, whatever B2 reads) and rule 10 (A read nothing there) B1 to `none`. Without B2, rules 5, 6 and 7 stay silent. `SecondRefusedError` is raised when the entities port refuses B2 (over 15,000 sub-tokens): the arbiter then decides the page without B2. Language tiers: French runs the entities and rule 4 in French; English, rule 4 in English and no entities; any other language, or none found, neither. `src/reading/phonemes.py` is espeak-ng through `phonemizer` (`espeakng-loader`, `fr-fr` and `en-us`, no stress, `language_switch="remove-utterance"`: a word espeak reads in another language has no phonemes and is no homophone), one call at a time, logging nothing. `src/reading/replay.py` (`python -m reading.replay` in the image) replays readings through the rules with the gate forced open: JSON on stdin (pages with B1, optionally B2, A's lines of words and confidences, language, level, and the entity flags of B1, of A and, with B2, of B2 pinned), positions on stdout; a bad input exits 2 with `invalid replay input`, never its content. The arbiter reads the correction level of the page's notebook only when the gate is open. `src/reading/entities.py` is the entities port: `distilcamembert-base-ner` in ONNX on the processor, one thread, read in windows of 510 sub-tokens on the form of the text (as the NER Spike did), `<` and `>` and their full-width and small-form look-alikes read as spaces, and any special id left after encoding read as `<unk>`, so that no special token can be written on a page, and above 15,000 sub-tokens for B1 or for A the arbitration answers 422 `too_complex`; a word is a proper noun when the first sub-token over its characters carries `PER`, `LOC`, `ORG` or `MISC`; one flag per token of `line_tokens`. onnxruntime 1.30 sends telemetry to Microsoft unless `ORT_DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` is set before it loads (the API switch comes too late): the image sets it, `Camembert` refuses to start without it and imports onnxruntime only after that check, and a test proves no connection opens. The service wires the rules at start (`Espeak` and `Camembert`) and does not start without `READING_NER`, the folder of the NER files. `src/reading/language.py` finds the language of B1 with lingua (all its languages, minimum relative distance 0.25), none under five words with a letter. `src/reading/second.py` ranges B2 by content before the aligner: `b2_content_map` of the Pairing Probe, same steps over the forms (case kept, apostrophes and dashes unified), then the global aligner of `Disagreement Spike/scripts/tokens.py` (`align_global`), so that each word of B1 has the word of B2 read there, or none; on 399 random pages and on the 52 stored pairs of real readings of the spikes it pairs exactly as the spike. A line of B1 (or of B2) is missing when none of its words is paired and its run of words is nowhere in the other pass. B2 is left out when it has no word, when it passes 1,000,000 cells or 50,000,000 letter pairs with B1, or when the search passes 20,000,000 steps (a page of identical words): the page is read as without B2, never refused. `src/reading/tokens.py` also gives `raw_spans`, the place of each word in the characters of the text as it was written (the NFC form `tokens_of` reads can be shorter); none for the whole page when a word would end inside a composed character. `src/reading/arbiter.py` reads the user's models, computes the positions and the language, gives each position its place in B1 (`b1_start`, `b1_end`) and the word B2 read there (`reading_b2`), gate open or not, and raises the `missing_line` alert of the page when one pass lacks a whole line (`absent_from_b2`, `absent_from_b1`: the indexes of those lines, 50 at most each, no text), gate open or not, uploads the ALTO to `alto/{user_id}/{page_id}/a-<32 hex>.xml` (a new name for each attempt), then calls `record_reading_a` (model `medium`, version `1`, the alerts in `p_alerts` only when there are some). It erases that file when the write is refused (409 with `page_not_found`, `not_text` or `already_read`) or surely failed (400, 401, 403, 404 or 409); after a failure that may have written (network, timeout, any other status) it leaves the file and logs `alto=left`. `src/reading/store.py` is the only code that calls Supabase: `htr_models`, the `correction_level` of a page's notebook (read through `pages`), the `alto` bucket and `record_reading_a`, with the service key, never `page_texts`, over https only (http only to `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `::1` or the stack's `supabase_kong_*` gateway, a name without a dot), following no redirect and no proxy of the environment. It answers 200 with `page_id`, `outcome` (`recorded`) and `tokens`, or 400 `invalid_request`, 409, 413 `too_large` (above 12 MB), 415, 422 `too_complex`, 503 `busy` (four arbitrations in flight), 500 `arbitration_failed`. Logs carry the page, durations and counts, never text or image. `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` pin every package: the Kraken chain keeps the versions that produced the Cold Spike (`constraint-dependencies`), torch comes from the PyTorch index for the processor. The `Dockerfile` downloads the `medium` model from Zenodo (record 21788410, Apache-2.0) and checks its SHA-256, and the NER the same way from Hugging Face at pinned revisions: `model.onnx` and `config.json` of `cmarkea/distilcamembert-base-ner` (MIT), `tokenizer.json` of `Xenova/camembert-ner` (the CamemBERT tokenizer the NER Spike used), into `/models/ner`; Kraken carries its `blla` segmenter; the service runs as a user that is not root and cannot change its model. Every route requires the shared secret in the `X-Reading-Secret` header, compared in constant time, and `Authorization` stays free for the identity token of Cloud Run; without a `READING_SECRET` of at least 32 characters the service does not start. Nor does it start without `SUPABASE_URL` (as above) and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`. `check.sh` runs ruff, mypy, pytest and pip-audit inside the image; `audit-allowlist.txt` lists the advisories pip-audit ignores. Both are guarded, and CI calls `check.sh` by name, so the default command of the image cannot skip it. `compose.yaml` runs it read-only with `/tmp` not executable; espeak copies its library into `READING_ESPEAK_DIR`, a small tmpfs of its own that allows it (`/run/espeak`); it runs on the network of the stack, at `http://notes-reading:8080`, and on `127.0.0.1:54340` for the PC only. Its local secret lives in `services/reading/.env`, ignored by git.
- `scripts/merge-pr.mjs`: the only way a PR is merged. `scripts/deploy-function.mjs`: the only way an Edge Function is deployed. Their rules live in `scripts/merge-gate.ts`, tested by `tests/merge-gate.test.ts`.
- `.githooks/pre-push`: refuses pushes to `main` and remote deletions.
- `docs/slice.md`: the state of the current slice.
- `.claude/agents/`: the `code-reviewer` (Sonnet) and `security-reviewer` (Opus by default; spawned with `model: "sonnet"` except on a critical branch) subagents. `.claude/settings.json`: the guardrails, tested by `tests/guardrails.test.ts`.

## Workflow

The owner does not read the code. What protects `main` is the spec, the tests, the checks and the fresh reviews below: skip none of them.

- One slice at a time. Its spec and its plan live in the owner's vault, outside the repository. `docs/slice.md` says where they are, which branches are done and which one is next.
- One session per branch. "Continue la tranche." means: read `docs/slice.md` and the slice document, do the next branch from start to merge, update `docs/slice.md`, and stop; the branch after it gets a new session. If `docs/slice.md` names no next branch, stop and ask.
- Each branch has a level in `docs/slice.md`: ordinary, sensitive (access rights, users' files, hostile input, deletion) or critical (accounts and authentication, secrets, exposure of a service, production, money). In doubt, the level above.
- Design the closed case first: allow-lists rather than deny-lists, deny by default, the smallest surface, explicit bounds on everything a user or a model writes (size, count, depth, time). Never a deny-list for hostile input.
- Tests that encode the criteria of the branch are written first and fail before the code exists.
- Before the PR, `typecheck`, `lint`, `test`, `audit`, `functions:check`, `functions:lint`, `functions:test`, `db:test`, `db:test:concurrency` and `service:test` pass locally (the last three need Docker; if it is off, say so and let CI run them).
- Review, one round only, no more agents than this: a fresh `code-reviewer` with the spec, the criteria of the branch and the branch name (paths, not pasted text). On a sensitive branch, also one fresh `security-reviewer` spawned with `model: "sonnet"`; on a critical branch, that `security-reviewer` on its default Opus. An ordinary branch gets the `code-reviewer` alone. A change that only moves dependency versions (CI and the audit already judge it) gets none. Fix the findings and check the fixes with the tests (a parallel test for locks), not with a second review; no second round, no extra agent. What is left goes in the PR as set aside with its reason, an important security risk left open also in "Pour Tim", and a blocking finding still open stops the session and is told to the owner.
- Keep the session light: read files by excerpts when possible, run the whole test suite once before the PR rather than after each edit.
- The PR explains every change in plain terms and ends with a `## Review` section: every finding, and what was done about it, fixed or set aside with the reason.
- Merge only with `node scripts/merge-pr.mjs <number>`, once CI has run on the last commit. It judges the PR with the gate of `main` (`scripts/merge-gate.ts`), never with the branch's own copy, and refuses while one of the five checks (`app`, `functions`, `database`, `secrets`, `reading`) is not green, while the branch lacks the last commit of `main`, without a `## Review` line, or when the PR changes a guarded file (`.claude/`, `.githooks/`, `.github/`, `scripts/`, the guardrail tests, `supabase/config.toml`, `audit-ci.json`, `services/reading/check.sh`, `services/reading/audit-allowlist.txt`, `.nvmrc`, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, `.npmrc`, the gitleaks, Babel, Jest and Deno configuration files, `eslint.config.js`, `tsconfig.json`, `tests/setup.ts`). In `package.json`, Claude merges only new, removed or updated versions of the npm registry in `dependencies` and `devDependencies`; any other key, a package that runs or shapes the checks (`expo`, `jest`, `jest-expo`, `typescript`, `eslint`, `eslint-config-expo`, `audit-ci`, `supabase`, `@testing-library/react-native`), or a lock entry outside the npm registry is the owner's. The guarded files are denied to the Edit and Write tools: never write them by another path (a shell, a script). When a branch needs one changed, prepare the change as a patch outside the repository and let the owner apply it. A PR that changes a guarded file is the owner's to merge: say so and stop. Never `gh pr merge`, never push to `main`, never rewrite history.
- The pre-push hook in `.githooks/` refuses any push to `main` and any deletion of a remote branch. It works once the owner has run `git config core.hooksPath .githooks` in the clone. At the start of a session, `git rev-parse --git-path hooks` must print `.githooks`; if not, stop and ask the owner. Create PRs with `--body-file`, so their text never looks like a denied command.
- The last commit of each branch updates `docs/slice.md`. After the merge, the session ends; when the branch was the last of the slice, it first writes the Bilan and the status below.
- Decisions: take alone what stays within the spec and a PR can undo (shape of a table, a limit, a free library, the order of the work, a fix from a review), and write each one, in French, one line with its reason, in `../Tranche N/Décisions.md`. The owner decides what costs money or creates an account, touches production, secrets or authentication settings, changes what the app promises or departs from the spec, or leaves a known security risk unfixed: write it in the "Pour Tim" section of the same file, one plain sentence with a proposal, take meanwhile the safest option that is easiest to undo, and go on with the branch. Stop only when nothing can move without the answer, or when a check is still red after two attempts to fix it.
- At the end of the slice, write `../Tranche N/Bilan.md` in French (what the slice does, in plain terms; its measures; what is left for the owner) and update the "Current Status" of `../../AGENTS.md`. Every third slice, also write the phone checks, step by step with the expected result, in `../Vérifications K.md`.

## Production

- Allowed: deploy an Edge Function with `node scripts/deploy-function.mjs <name>`. It deploys only a clean copy of the last commit of `main` whose CI is green, with the token check that `supabase/config.toml` sets. It is undone by a PR that reverts the change, merged, then deployed the same way. Cloud Run deploys come with their own script, in the branch that creates the service, merged by the owner.
- The owner's alone: production migrations, secrets, authentication settings, who may call a service and public access, billing, accounts and projects, and every `gcloud` command until that script exists.
- `.claude/settings.json` denies these commands in both shells, the Supabase tools that write, the browser and computer-use tools, reading `supabase/functions/.env` and `services/reading/.env`, and editing the guarded files. `tests/guardrails.test.ts` fails if a rule is added or removed without the test, if an allowed command becomes blocked, or if a known bypass stops being blocked.
- These guardrails stop mistakes, not a determined agent: a deny list matches the text of a command, and a shell can still write any file or read `supabase/functions/.env` by another path (`grep -r` on `supabase/functions` reads it too: search `supabase/functions/*/` instead). The secret of the reading service is in the environment of its container: `docker inspect`, `docker exec` and `docker compose config` are denied for that reason; read its logs with `docker logs notes-reading`. The gate of `main`, the pre-push hook and the owner merging guarded files are the parts that do not depend on the agent's goodwill.
- Never read page text or images from production.

## Security rules

- Row level security on every table, deny by default, `user_id = auth.uid()` and only the operations the client is allowed. Every policy has a test proving a user can neither read nor modify another user's data. `rls.test.sql` fails if a table in `public` has RLS disabled.
- A new table in `public` gets no right by default, locally and in production: every migration that creates one grants its rights explicitly, to `authenticated` column by column and to `service_role` (`select, insert, update, delete`). `tests/server-grants.test.ts` and `server-grants.test.sql` fail otherwise.
- `profiles` has no `user_id`: its key `id` is the account id and its rule is `id = auth.uid()`. Tables written only by the server have no default for `user_id`: the server always gives it.
- The client only writes notebooks, chapters, pages, corrections, consents, its interface language, birth year and country. Everything else is written by the server.
- The client deletes no row and changes neither `pages.notebook_id` nor `pages.chapter_id`: a page is moved and a page, a chapter or a notebook is deleted only by `move_page`, `delete_page`, `delete_chapter` and `delete_notebook` (server only, the owner given by the verified token). Each answers an outcome (`moved`, `unchanged`, `deleted`, `page_not_found`, `chapter_not_found`, `notebook_not_found`, `page_processing`; `delete_notebook` also answers the ids of the pages it deleted), refuses a page being read, and takes the advisory lock of the user first (the one `charge_page` and the transfer take), then its row locks notebook first, then chapter, then pages by id (`supabase/tests/concurrency/library-race.sh` plays them all at once). A chapter name is unique in its notebook; deleting a chapter keeps its pages, out of any chapter; deleting a notebook deletes its pages, and the charges of deleted pages stay with no page. The functions touch no file: the Edge Function erases the files first. A transfer merges chapters of the same name like notebooks. Every foreign key `(…, user_id)` has an index starting with its columns.
- Storage buckets are private; a user only reads photos under their own `user_id`. `alto` (the ALTO files of A, `application/xml`, 5 MB at most) and `models` (personal HTR models) have no rule at all: only the server reads or writes them. A file of a page lives under `{user_id}/{page_id}/`, a model under `{user_id}/`.
- Reading A is written only by `record_reading_a` (server only): the reading, its positions in `page_tokens`, `pages.language` and the `missing_line` alert of the page (`p_alerts`: no other kind, one of a kind a page, one already there kept as it is), in one transaction, the page row locked. It answers `recorded`, or refuses with `page_not_found` (no such page, or another owner), `not_text` or `already_read`; on a refusal or an error the caller erases the ALTO file it uploaded. A reading A has an `alto_path` (a file name directly in its page folder) and none of the settings of a Gemini call, B1 and B2 the reverse; a page has one A at most. A page leaves `text` only once its A and its positions are erased (trigger `private.check_page_type`).
- Corrections and page alerts (slice 4). `page_tokens` also holds `reading_b2`, `review_status` (`pending`, `confirmed`, `corrected`, `skipped`, only for a position of the `queue` channel, which starts `pending`) and the place of the word in the raw text of B1 (`b1_start` included, `b1_end` excluded, in characters; a place never overlaps another and follows the rank, a page has all its places or none; the server writes them through `record_reading_a`, whose token objects may carry `reading_b2`, `b1_start` and `b1_end`). The text a page shows (`page_texts.text`) is rebuilt by `private.recompute_page_text` from the B1 reading, the places and, for each position, its last correction, else the word a rule wrote, else the slice of B1 as it was, so that line breaks and punctuation never change; a page whose positions have no place keeps its text. The client writes `corrections` (insert of `page_id`, `token_index`, `corrected_text`, `chosen_from`, `from_queue` only, no update, no delete): `private.prepare_correction` locks the page, gives the `revision` (1 to 100 a position, 2,000 corrections a page) and `previous_text`, keeps `from_queue` only on a pending position, and refuses a word that is not one word of 1 to 100 characters (no space, control, bidirectional or invisible character from its fixed list), a position that has no place, and a `chosen_from` of `A`, `B1` or `B2` whose word is not exactly that reading of the position; `private.apply_correction` moves a pending queue position to `confirmed` or `corrected` and recalculates the text. Undoing a correction adds one that returns to the previous word. `page_alerts` (`date_incoherent`, `missing_line`, `math_incomplete`, one of each kind a page, `details` an object of 16 KB at most) is written by the server only (`math_incomplete` only by `record_math_alert`, which refuses a page that is not math or lacks B1 or B2, and counts that are negative or both zero). A page has at most one reading B2 (`readings_one_b2_per_page`). `set_page_math` erases the positions (and with them the corrections), the `missing_line` alert and A, and gives the text of B1 back. `supabase/tests/concurrency/corrections-race.sh` plays 20 corrections at once on one page.
- Text of the user (slice 4, `t4/edit`). `page_gaps` (`page_id`, `gap_index`, `text` of 100,000 characters at most; read by its owner, written by the server only) holds the text between the words of a page: gap `n` is what comes before the position of rank `n` (counted from 0), the last gap what comes after the last position, and on a page of math, which has no position, the whole text in gap 0. `private.recompute_page_text` takes the gap in place of the slice of B1 when there is one, and rebuilds a page of math from its gap 0 or from B1. A correction may be empty (a deleted word) only when the server writes it (`auth.uid()` empty): a client's empty correction is refused. `edit_page(page, owner, text, words, gaps)` (server only, owner given by the verified token) is the only way the text of the user is written, and `set_page_math` and `set_page_text` erase it and show B1 again. `supabase/tests/concurrency/edit-race.sh` plays edits and corrections at once on one page (like `corrections-race.sh`, in `db:test:concurrency` only once the owner applies the patch of `t4-schema package.json.patch`).
- `pages.page_type` (`text` by default) and `pages.language` (two lowercase letters, or nothing) are written by the server only: by `read-page` and `relaunch-page` with B1, and by `set_page_math` and `set_page_text` (server only) through `set-page-type`. `notebooks.correction_level` stays `course` unless its owner has an `active` personal model in `htr_models` (trigger `private.check_correction_level`), and the client never writes it. `htr_models` holds the generic `medium` (no owner, no file: it is in the image) and, from slice 5, personal models; `cer` is a fraction (0.129 is 12.9 %).
- No secret in the repository. Secrets live in Supabase Edge Function secrets or Google Secret Manager; locally, in `supabase/functions/.env` and `services/reading/.env`, both ignored by git and never copied into the image (`.dockerignore` lets only the listed files in).
- The reading service is never public: it refuses every call without the shared secret, and locally it is published on `127.0.0.1` only, never on the local network. The app only holds the Supabase URL and publishable key. The app reaches production through `.env.development.local`, ignored by git, holding only the production URL and publishable key; renamed `.env.development.local.off`, the app is back on the local stack.
- Logs never contain page text or images.
- Model output is text, never code: never executed, rendered without HTML or active links.
- Usage limits are enforced by Edge Functions, never by the app.
- An e-mail address is kept by an account only once its code is proven (`enable_confirmations = true`, local and production).
- No account is created in advance for an address: the hook `private.refuse_email_signup` (`[auth.hook.before_user_created]`, local and production) refuses every new account that comes from an e-mail or a phone number. Without it, anyone could create an account for someone else's address with a password they know, and keep access to it once the owner signs in by code. The signup settings stay open: turning off `enable_signup` blocks the anonymous accounts, turning off the e-mail one blocks the sign-in by code.
- `delete-account` takes the account from the verified token alone, never from the request, erases the files of the account in `photos`, `alto` and `models` before the account, then `alto` once more (an A that ended meanwhile; a failure there is logged as `account_files_left`, the deletion stands), and logs no identifier.
- `transfer-account` takes both accounts from the two verified tokens alone (the first must be anonymous, the second registered), never from the request. It copies the photos, moves the rows in one transaction (`transfer_account_rows`, which rewrites the paths of photos and ALTO files), copies the ALTO files (after the rows, so that an A written meanwhile is not left behind), then erases the old files of the three buckets and the anonymous account; every step can be played again. It refuses while a page of the anonymous account is being read, is never stopped by a usage limit, and logs no identifier.
