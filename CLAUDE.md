# NotesScannerApp

iOS app that turns photos of handwritten notes into faithful digital text. It never invents text: fidelity to the page comes before readability.

## Stack

- App: Expo SDK 57, React Native 0.86, React 19, TypeScript strict. Expo has changed: read the versioned docs at https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/ before writing app code.
- Backend: Supabase (Postgres, Auth, Storage, Edge Functions in TypeScript on Deno), Paris region.
- Reading model: `gemini-3.6-flash`, prompt V0, default temperature, `thinkingBudget: 0`, called only from Edge Functions.
- Reading service: Python 3.13 in `services/reading/` (FastAPI, Kraken 7.1.1, PyTorch for the processor only), one Docker image. It runs on the PC next to the local Supabase stack until Cloud Run, at the end of slice 4.

## Layout

- `src/app/`: routes (Expo Router) and their `_layout.tsx`, nothing else.
- `src/components/`: shared components.
- `src/i18n/`: language files (`en.ts`, `fr.ts`) and the choice of language.
- `src/auth/`: the anonymous account, the provider that opens it when the app starts and follows every change of account (sign-up, sign-in, sign-out), and the actions of the account by e-mail code (`account.ts`, `account-errors.ts`, `account-service.ts`). A sign-in from an anonymous account that has pages checks its code on a second client that keeps nothing (`getStatelessSupabase`), calls `transfer-account` with both tokens, and only then gives the account found to the client of the phone.
- `src/lib/`: the Supabase client, its config, the secure session storage, the addresses of the terms and the privacy policy (`legal-links.ts`), the opening of a web address (`open-link.ts`) and the forgetting of the data of other accounts.
- `src/notebooks/`: reading, creating and caching notebooks.
- `src/capture/`: the limits shown before the camera and the hook that opens the camera or the limit sheet, sending a page (create, prepare, upload, mark it uploaded, start the reading), preparing the photo, the camera and network hooks. The photo is saved again in the sense of the viewfinder, never in the sense the phone guessed from gravity: the camera gives its orientation tag (`exif: true`), the app turns the image back to the viewfinder (plus the quarter turns of "Rotate"), shrinks it above 12 Mpx, and saves one JPEG with no metadata. The viewfinder and the check of the photo have the shape of the photo (3:4). A page only counts for the user (notebook counts, last notebook, page lists) once its `uploaded_at` is set, so a page created for a photo that never arrived stays invisible.
- `src/pages/`: the pages a user sees (list, one page with its number and text, signed address of its photo, starting or restarting a reading), what a page shows according to its status, the title of a page and the gluing of its cut lines at display time (the text itself is never changed), dates without `Intl`, and the polling delays. Every query here keeps to pages whose `uploaded_at` is set. `formulas.ts` cuts the body of a page of math into text and formulas (`$…$` on one line with no space inside its dollars, `$$…$$`, `\(…\)`, `\[…\]`, `\begin{…}…\end{…}`; anything unclosed stays text) and into rows; `tex.ts` draws one formula as an SVG string with MathJax 4 (`@mathjax/src` and its font, Apache-2.0) on the phone, with the TeX packages `base` and `ams` only (no `\href`, `\url`, `\class`, `\style`, `\require`), every font range it may need imported with the app so nothing is ever fetched, and an output with a link or an image refused; it refuses before drawing a formula over 2,000 characters, nested deeper than 20 (counted with what is open: a brace, a `\left` until its `\right`, an environment until its `\end`), using `\DeclareMathOperator` or `\pmb` (their nesting doubles the work), `\mmlToken` (MathML attributes), `\newcolumntype`, `\label`, `\tag`, `\ref`, `\eqref`, `\hsize`, `\vbox`, `\vtop`, `\vcenter` or `\parbox` (the last five start a slow line breaking), an environment outside the common ones (the matrices, `cases`, `aligned`, `align`, `gathered`, `gather`, `equation`, `split`, `multline`, `flalign`, `eqnarray`, `array`; so no `alignat` nor `subarray`), an `array` whose column template is not 1 to 20 columns `l`, `c` or `r` with at most one `|` around each, a `\begin` whose name cannot be read, or a `%` comment (it would hide what it closes from the count); after drawing it refuses an SVG over 512 KB, larger than 300 by 120 ex or 4,000 square ex, of more than 3,000 elements, or holding a link, an image or a `url(`. Each formula gets a fresh TeX input and a fresh SVG output, so nothing it defines or sets reaches the next; the drawing must be one root picture (inline line breaks are off). Formulas are drawn one at a time, each after a turn of the screen, a formula asked twice waits for the first drawing, a drawing still queued when its formula leaves the screen is dropped (`releaseTex`), a page draws at most 100 formulas and shows the rest as read, and the last 50 results are kept (the oldest goes first) and read at once. A formula it refuses is shown as it was read, never as an error. The engine itself (`mathjax-engine.ts`) is loaded on the first formula, not at app start. On a page of math the first line is the title only when no formula starts on it; display formulas scroll sideways when wider than the screen, and an inline formula wider than 36 ex is shown as read. `mathjax-context.ts`, imported first, gives React Native's `navigator` the `appVersion` and `userAgent` MathJax reads at load. `metro.config.js` resolves the package subpath imports of MathJax (`#default-font/*` and kin), whose bare targets Metro 0.84 cannot follow.
- `src/components/account/`: the groups and rows of the account screen.
- `src/components/reading/`: the screens' parts for a reading: ink stroke, waiting view, document, original photo with its zoom, page rows and cards. Under the title of a read document, a page of text or of math shows the "math page" switch, which calls `set-page-type` and comes back to the stored type when the call fails; a page in an unsupported script shows instead a sentence saying the reading AI alone reads it. The body of a page of math is drawn with its formulas (`formula.tsx`, through `react-native-svg`, labelled with its LaTeX for screen readers), the rest of the text in Source Serif 4; a page of text keeps its LaTeX as it was read.
- `src/theme.ts`: colors, fonts and type sizes of the design direction.
- `tests/`: Jest tests. `tests/fixtures/b1-line-shapes.json` holds only the shape of the lines of the 18 B1 readings of the Thinking Spike (every letter is `x` or `X`, every digit `0`), with the verdict of the ground truth for each line break; no text of a page may enter the repository. `tests/google-keys.test.ts` reads every file git would send (tracked, or new and not ignored) and fails on a Google key or identifier, naming the file and the line, never the value.
- `supabase/migrations/`: the only way the schema changes.
- `supabase/templates/`: the e-mail templates (sign-up, sign-in, confirmation). Each one shows `{{ .Token }}`, the code, and never a link; `config.toml` references them and sets the code at 6 digits, valid 10 minutes, a new one after 30 seconds, and the proof of the address before an account keeps it.
- `supabase/functions/`: Edge Functions (`prepare-page`, `read-page`, `relaunch-page`, `set-page-type`, `delete-account`, `transfer-account`), their shared code in `_shared/`, their Deno tests in `tests/`. `set-page-type` switches a read page of its caller (the owner from the verified token alone) between `text` and `math`; the body holds exactly `page_id` and `page_type` (`text` or `math`, never `unsupported_script`). To math, `set_page_math` (server only, the page row locked) erases A, its positions and the language and answers the ALTO path, which the function erases (a failure is logged `alto_left`, the switch stands). To text, `set_page_text` sets the type and answers B1 (its one `B1` reading), and A is read again in the background from the stored photo, as in `read-page`: no charge, no Gemini. A is read again at most once in ten minutes and three times in all for a page (`pages.a_relaunches`, `pages.a_relaunched_at`, written under the same lock, never by the client); past that the type still switches and no A starts (`a_abandoned relaunch_limit`); without a reading service configured, the switch counts nothing (`a_abandoned no_service`). A photo that is missing, not a JPEG or cannot be downloaded leaves the page text without A, and the switch is answered 200. A page not read, in an unsupported script or of another owner is refused; a switch to the type the page has changes nothing. An A that ends after a switch to math is refused by `record_reading_a` (`not_text`). `read-page` and `relaunch-page` share one reading (`_shared/reading.ts`): once the page is claimed, its photo goes at the same time to Gemini (B1) and to `/recognize` of the reading service (A, `_shared/reading-a.ts`). B1 decides the type of the page (`_shared/page-type.ts`: `math` when it holds a LaTeX delimiter or math command, `$…$` only on one line and when the opening `$` is followed and the closing `$` preceded by a character that is not a space, `$$…$$` when it holds a character that is not a space, `unsupported_script` when most of its letters are not Latin, else `text`), written with `read` in one update; the answer goes back without waiting for A, which goes on in the background (`EdgeRuntime.waitUntil`). On a text page A and B1 go to `/arbitrate`; on any other type, or when B1 fails, A is cancelled and nothing is written. A failure that may pass (network, timeout, 429, 5xx) is tried once more, a refusal never; then the page stays with B1 alone. `_shared/reading-service.ts` is the only code that calls the service: the secret in `X-Reading-Secret`, no redirect, https, or http only when `SUPABASE_URL` is itself http (the local stack), to `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `::1` or a name without a dot, and with no proxy of the environment on the way; the budget of A is 140 s from the send, a timeout of `/recognize` or of `/arbitrate` is never tried again (the call goes on in the service); the answer of `/recognize` passed on as it came. Without `READING_URL` and `READING_SECRET` the page is read by B1 alone; with one of them only, a secret under 32 characters or with spaces around it, an address that is not an origin alone or not https (outside the local stack), or a proxy that would see the secret, the page is read by B1 alone too and the start logs `reading_service_misconfigured` with a fixed code, never a value. Local secrets (`GEMINI_API_KEY`, `RELAUNCH_SECRET`, `READING_URL`, `READING_SECRET`) live in `supabase/functions/.env`, ignored by git. The `pg_cron` task that calls `relaunch-page` reads its secret and address from Supabase Vault, never from the repository.
- `supabase/tests/database/`: pgTAP tests.
- `supabase/tests/concurrency/`: parallel-call tests.
- `services/reading/`: the reading service. `src/reading/segmentation.py` cuts a photo into lines with the chain of the Frame Spike, every setting unchanged: Kraken's `blla` finds baseline fragments, fragments of the same height make one row, `ink.py` keeps the ink components under a threshold set from the page's own ink (so a printed grid goes and a light pencil stroke stays), each component goes to its row (a component across two rows is cut), and each row becomes one straightened grey tile at most 1,700 px wide, its neighbours' ink erased, rows in order from the top. Each line keeps its ink box on the photo and the placement that carries a point of the tile back to the photo (continuous coordinates: the centre of pixel `(u, v)` is `(u + 0.5, v + 0.5)`). `blla` runs on the processor and on one thread, as `kraken segment` does by default, so the lines of a photo do not depend on the number of cores; the pinned tiles of the spikes came from `blla` on a graphics card, so 77 of their 310 tiles differ slightly from the service's (measure in `04 Testing/T2 mesures/segmentation/`, outside the repository). The thread limit is process-wide: keep segmentation off threads that run other work. `src/reading/recognition.py` reads each tile as one whole line with `medium` on the processor, one thread, as `kraken ocr --no-segmentation` did for the Cold Spike; a word is a run between spaces, its box runs from the cut of its first character to the cut of its last (one tile pixel wide for a one-letter word), carried onto the photo, and its confidence is the mean of its characters rounded to 4 decimals, as Kraken's ALTO writes it. `Reader` reads one page at a time. `POST /recognize?page_id=<lowercase uuid>` takes the photo as the raw body with exactly one `Content-Type: image/jpeg`, and nothing else (any other query field or body type is refused), and answers `page_id`, `width`, `height`, `lines` (`row`, `box` of the tile on the photo, `ink` box, `words` with `text`, `box`, `confidence`) and `alto`; it writes nothing. Refusals have fixed bodies: 400 `invalid_request` or `invalid_photo` (not baseline or progressive, more than 16 scans, broken), 413 `too_large` (above 10 MB, 12 Mpx or 8,000 px a side), 415 `unsupported_media_type`, 422 `too_complex` (more than 40,000 ink components, or more than 120 s of reading), 503 `busy` (four requests already in flight), 500 `reading_failed`. `POST /arbitrate` takes exactly one `Content-Type: application/json` and a body of exactly `page_id`, `user_id` (lowercase uuids), `b1` (the text B1 shows), `a` (the answer of `/recognize` as it came, its ALTO checked against the ALTO its lines give) and `duration_ms`; it needs no Kraken work and does not wait for a reading. `src/reading/tokens.py` cuts a text into words as `Disagreement Spike/scripts/tokens.py` does (same matches, same words left out), but keeps each word as written in B1 (case, apostrophes, hyphens) and compares a form where apostrophes and dashes are unified, case kept. `src/reading/alignment.py` is the block aligner of `Signal Spike/scripts/align_blocks.py` (`align_free`), unchanged: B1 is the pivot, a line `/recognize` cut but read empty is a declared hole, a hole costs nothing and absorbs at most the surplus of words of B1 over A (and the length of as many of B1's longest lines); a page above 1,000,000 cells of the table, or whose letters of B1 times letters of A pass 50,000,000, answers 422 `too_complex`. `src/reading/arbitration.py` gives one position per word of B1: its rank, its line in B1, the word of A paired with it, and its anchor: the box of that word of A; else the `box` of the line of A nearest on the same line of B1; else, in a hole, the line of the hole (one line of B1 per line of the hole, or the box spanning the hole when their numbers differ); else none. The gate is open only on an `active` personal model whose `cer` is at most 0.129; closed, no rule runs and `rule` and `channel` stay empty. `src/reading/rules.py` holds the rules that need only A and B1, gate open, in the order 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10 of the Arbitrage: the type is the strictest of A and B1 (a digit in either makes a number; a proper noun flagged by the entities port on B1 or on the word of A paired with it); readings are compared in their form (case kept, apostrophes and dashes unified); rule 2 (number read differently) writes B1 to `queue`; rule 3 (proper noun) B1 to `layer`; rule 4 (common word, A at a confidence of at least 0.990, one edit, homophones by the phonemes port, not an accent alone, not a truncation) A on a `journal` notebook, else B1, to `layer`; rule 8 (any other disagreement) B1 to `layer`; rule 9 (A and B1 agree) and rule 10 (A read nothing there) B1 to `none`. Language tiers: French runs the entities and rule 4 in French; English, rule 4 in English and no entities; any other language, or none found, neither. `src/reading/phonemes.py` is espeak-ng through `phonemizer` (`espeakng-loader`, `fr-fr` and `en-us`, no stress, `language_switch="remove-utterance"`: a word espeak reads in another language has no phonemes and is no homophone), one call at a time, logging nothing. `src/reading/replay.py` (`python -m reading.replay` in the image) replays readings through the rules with the gate forced open: JSON on stdin (pages with B1, A's lines of words and confidences, language, level, and the entity flags of B1 and of A pinned), positions on stdout; a bad input exits 2 with `invalid replay input`, never its content. The arbiter reads the correction level of the page's notebook only when the gate is open. `src/reading/entities.py` is the entities port: `distilcamembert-base-ner` in ONNX on the processor, one thread, read in windows of 510 sub-tokens on the form of the text (as the NER Spike did), `<` and `>` and their full-width and small-form look-alikes read as spaces, and any special id left after encoding read as `<unk>`, so that no special token can be written on a page, and above 15,000 sub-tokens for B1 or for A the arbitration answers 422 `too_complex`; a word is a proper noun when the first sub-token over its characters carries `PER`, `LOC`, `ORG` or `MISC`; one flag per token of `line_tokens`. onnxruntime 1.30 sends telemetry to Microsoft unless `ORT_DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1` is set before it loads (the API switch comes too late): the image sets it, `Camembert` refuses to start without it and imports onnxruntime only after that check, and a test proves no connection opens. The service wires the rules at start (`Espeak` and `Camembert`) and does not start without `READING_NER`, the folder of the NER files. `src/reading/language.py` finds the language of B1 with lingua (all its languages, minimum relative distance 0.25), none under five words with a letter. `src/reading/arbiter.py` reads the user's models, computes the positions and the language, uploads the ALTO to `alto/{user_id}/{page_id}/a-<32 hex>.xml` (a new name for each attempt), then calls `record_reading_a` (model `medium`, version `1`). It erases that file when the write is refused (409 with `page_not_found`, `not_text` or `already_read`) or surely failed (400, 401, 403, 404 or 409); after a failure that may have written (network, timeout, any other status) it leaves the file and logs `alto=left`. `src/reading/store.py` is the only code that calls Supabase: `htr_models`, the `correction_level` of a page's notebook (read through `pages`), the `alto` bucket and `record_reading_a`, with the service key, never `page_texts`, over https only (http only to `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `::1` or the stack's `supabase_kong_*` gateway, a name without a dot), following no redirect and no proxy of the environment. It answers 200 with `page_id`, `outcome` (`recorded`) and `tokens`, or 400 `invalid_request`, 409, 413 `too_large` (above 12 MB), 415, 422 `too_complex`, 503 `busy` (four arbitrations in flight), 500 `arbitration_failed`. Logs carry the page, durations and counts, never text or image. `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` pin every package: the Kraken chain keeps the versions that produced the Cold Spike (`constraint-dependencies`), torch comes from the PyTorch index for the processor. The `Dockerfile` downloads the `medium` model from Zenodo (record 21788410, Apache-2.0) and checks its SHA-256, and the NER the same way from Hugging Face at pinned revisions: `model.onnx` and `config.json` of `cmarkea/distilcamembert-base-ner` (MIT), `tokenizer.json` of `Xenova/camembert-ner` (the CamemBERT tokenizer the NER Spike used), into `/models/ner`; Kraken carries its `blla` segmenter; the service runs as a user that is not root and cannot change its model. Every route requires the shared secret in the `X-Reading-Secret` header, compared in constant time, and `Authorization` stays free for the identity token of Cloud Run; without a `READING_SECRET` of at least 32 characters the service does not start. Nor does it start without `SUPABASE_URL` (as above) and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`. `check.sh` runs ruff, mypy, pytest and pip-audit inside the image; `audit-allowlist.txt` lists the advisories pip-audit ignores. Both are guarded, and CI calls `check.sh` by name, so the default command of the image cannot skip it. `compose.yaml` runs it read-only with `/tmp` not executable; espeak copies its library into `READING_ESPEAK_DIR`, a small tmpfs of its own that allows it (`/run/espeak`); it runs on the network of the stack, at `http://notes-reading:8080`, and on `127.0.0.1:54340` for the PC only. Its local secret lives in `services/reading/.env`, ignored by git.
- `scripts/merge-pr.mjs`: the only way a PR is merged. `scripts/deploy-function.mjs`: the only way an Edge Function is deployed. Their rules live in `scripts/merge-gate.ts`, tested by `tests/merge-gate.test.ts`.
- `.githooks/pre-push`: refuses pushes to `main` and remote deletions.
- `docs/slice.md`: the state of the current slice.
- `.claude/agents/`: the `code-reviewer` (Sonnet) and `security-reviewer` (Opus by default; spawned with `model: "sonnet"` except on a critical branch) subagents. `.claude/settings.json`: the guardrails, tested by `tests/guardrails.test.ts`.

## Workflow

The owner does not read the code. What protects `main` is the spec, the tests, the checks and the fresh reviews below: skip none of them.

- One slice at a time. Its spec and its plan live in the owner's vault, outside the repository. `docs/slice.md` says where they are, which branches are done and which one is next.
- One session per branch. "Continue la tranche." means: read `docs/slice.md` and the slice document, do the next branch from start to merge, update `docs/slice.md`, and stop; the branch after it gets a new session. If `docs/slice.md` names no next branch, stop and ask.
- Each branch has a level in `docs/slice.md`: ordinary, sensitive (access rights, users' files, hostile input, deletion) or critical (accounts and authentication, secrets, exposure of a service, production, money). In doubt, the level above.
- Design the closed case first: allow-lists rather than deny-lists, deny by default, the smallest surface, explicit bounds on everything a user or a model writes (size, count, depth, time). Never a deny-list for hostile input.
- Tests that encode the criteria of the branch are written first and fail before the code exists.
- Before the PR, `typecheck`, `lint`, `test`, `audit`, `functions:check`, `functions:lint`, `functions:test`, `db:test`, `db:test:concurrency` and `service:test` pass locally (the last three need Docker; if it is off, say so and let CI run them).
- Review, first round: a fresh `code-reviewer` with the spec, the criteria of the branch and the branch name (paths, not pasted text). On a sensitive branch, also a fresh `security-reviewer` spawned with `model: "sonnet"`; on a critical branch, a fresh `security-reviewer` on its default Opus. Fix the findings. Second round, only when a fix answered a blocking or important finding: one fresh `code-reviewer` on the fixes alone. No third round: what is left goes in the PR as set aside with its reason, an important security risk left open also in "Pour Tim", and a blocking finding still open stops the session and is told to the owner.
- Keep the session light: read files by excerpts when possible, run the whole test suite once before the PR rather than after each edit.
- The PR explains every change in plain terms and ends with a `## Review` section: every finding, and what was done about it, fixed or set aside with the reason.
- Merge only with `node scripts/merge-pr.mjs <number>`, once CI has run on the last commit. It judges the PR with the gate of `main` (`scripts/merge-gate.ts`), never with the branch's own copy, and refuses while one of the five checks (`app`, `functions`, `database`, `secrets`, `reading`) is not green, while the branch lacks the last commit of `main`, without a `## Review` line, or when the PR changes a guarded file (`.claude/`, `.githooks/`, `.github/`, `scripts/`, the guardrail tests, `supabase/config.toml`, `audit-ci.json`, `services/reading/check.sh`, `services/reading/audit-allowlist.txt`, `.nvmrc`, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, `.npmrc`, the gitleaks, Babel, Jest and Deno configuration files, `eslint.config.js`, `tsconfig.json`, `tests/setup.ts`). In `package.json`, Claude merges only new, removed or updated versions of the npm registry in `dependencies` and `devDependencies`; any other key, a package that runs or shapes the checks (`expo`, `jest`, `jest-expo`, `typescript`, `eslint`, `eslint-config-expo`, `audit-ci`, `supabase`, `@testing-library/react-native`), or a lock entry outside the npm registry is the owner's. The guarded files are denied to the Edit and Write tools: never write them by another path (a shell, a script). When a branch needs one changed, prepare the change as a patch outside the repository and let the owner apply it. A PR that changes a guarded file is the owner's to merge: say so and stop. Never `gh pr merge`, never push to `main`, never rewrite history.
- The pre-push hook in `.githooks/` refuses any push to `main` and any deletion of a remote branch. It works once the owner has run `git config core.hooksPath .githooks` in the clone. At the start of a session, `git rev-parse --git-path hooks` must print `.githooks`; if not, stop and ask the owner. Create PRs with `--body-file`, so their text never looks like a denied command.
- The last commit of each branch updates `docs/slice.md`. After the merge, the session ends; when the branch was the last of the slice, it first writes the Bilan and the status below.
- Decisions: take alone what stays within the spec and a PR can undo (shape of a table, a limit, a free library, the order of the work, a fix from a review), and write each one, in French, one line with its reason, in `../Tranche N/Décisions.md`. The owner decides what costs money or creates an account, touches production, secrets or authentication settings, changes what the app promises or departs from the spec, or leaves a known security risk unfixed: write it in the "Pour Tim" section of the same file, one plain sentence with a proposal, take meanwhile the safest option that is easiest to undo, and go on with the branch. Stop only when nothing can move without the answer, or when a check is still red after two attempts to fix it.
- At the end of the slice, write `../Tranche N/Bilan.md` in French (what the slice does, in plain terms; its measures; what is left for the owner) and update the "Current Status" of `../../AGENTS.md`. Every third slice, also write the phone checks, step by step with the expected result, in `../Vérifications K.md`.

## Production

- Allowed: deploy an Edge Function with `node scripts/deploy-function.mjs <name>`. It deploys only a clean copy of the last commit of `main` whose CI is green, with the token check that `supabase/config.toml` sets. It is undone by a PR that reverts the change, merged, then deployed the same way. Cloud Run deploys come with their own script, in the branch that creates the service, merged by the owner.
- The owner's alone: production migrations, secrets, authentication settings, who may call a service and public access, billing, accounts and projects, and every `gcloud` command until that script exists.
- `.claude/settings.json` denies these commands in both shells, the Supabase tools that write, the browser and computer-use tools, reading `supabase/functions/.env` and `services/reading/.env`, and editing the guarded files. `tests/guardrails.test.ts` fails if a rule is added or removed without the test, if an allowed command becomes blocked, or if a known bypass stops being blocked.
- These guardrails stop mistakes, not a determined agent: a deny list matches the text of a command, and a shell can still write any file or read `supabase/functions/.env` by another path (`grep -r` on `supabase/functions` reads it too: search `supabase/functions/*/` instead). The secret of the reading service is in the environment of its container: `docker inspect`, `docker exec` and `docker compose config` are denied for that reason; read its logs with `docker logs notes-reading`. The gate of `main`, the pre-push hook and the owner merging guarded files are the parts that do not depend on the agent's goodwill.
- Never read page text or images from production.

## Security rules

- Row level security on every table, deny by default, `user_id = auth.uid()` and only the operations the client is allowed. Every policy has a test proving a user can neither read nor modify another user's data. `rls.test.sql` fails if a table in `public` has RLS disabled.
- A new table in `public` gets no right by default, locally and in production: every migration that creates one grants its rights explicitly, to `authenticated` column by column and to `service_role` (`select, insert, update, delete`). `tests/server-grants.test.ts` and `server-grants.test.sql` fail otherwise.
- `profiles` has no `user_id`: its key `id` is the account id and its rule is `id = auth.uid()`. Tables written only by the server have no default for `user_id`: the server always gives it.
- The client only writes notebooks, chapters, pages, corrections, consents, its interface language, birth year and country. Everything else is written by the server.
- Storage buckets are private; a user only reads photos under their own `user_id`. `alto` (the ALTO files of A, `application/xml`, 5 MB at most) and `models` (personal HTR models) have no rule at all: only the server reads or writes them. A file of a page lives under `{user_id}/{page_id}/`, a model under `{user_id}/`.
- Reading A is written only by `record_reading_a` (server only): the reading, its positions in `page_tokens` and `pages.language`, in one transaction, the page row locked. It answers `recorded`, or refuses with `page_not_found` (no such page, or another owner), `not_text` or `already_read`; on a refusal or an error the caller erases the ALTO file it uploaded. A reading A has an `alto_path` (a file name directly in its page folder) and none of the settings of a Gemini call, B1 and B2 the reverse; a page has one A at most. A page leaves `text` only once its A and its positions are erased (trigger `private.check_page_type`).
- `pages.page_type` (`text` by default) and `pages.language` (two lowercase letters, or nothing) are written by the server only: by `read-page` and `relaunch-page` with B1, and by `set_page_math` and `set_page_text` (server only) through `set-page-type`. `notebooks.correction_level` stays `course` unless its owner has an `active` personal model in `htr_models` (trigger `private.check_correction_level`), and the client never writes it. `htr_models` holds the generic `medium` (no owner, no file: it is in the image) and, from slice 5, personal models; `cer` is a fraction (0.129 is 12.9 %).
- No secret in the repository. Secrets live in Supabase Edge Function secrets or Google Secret Manager; locally, in `supabase/functions/.env` and `services/reading/.env`, both ignored by git and never copied into the image (`.dockerignore` lets only the listed files in).
- The reading service is never public: it refuses every call without the shared secret, and locally it is published on `127.0.0.1` only, never on the local network. The app only holds the Supabase URL and publishable key. The app reaches production through `.env.development.local`, ignored by git, holding only the production URL and publishable key; renamed `.env.development.local.off`, the app is back on the local stack.
- Logs never contain page text or images.
- Model output is text, never code: never executed, rendered without HTML or active links.
- Usage limits are enforced by Edge Functions, never by the app.
- An e-mail address is kept by an account only once its code is proven (`enable_confirmations = true`, local and production).
- No account is created in advance for an address: the hook `private.refuse_email_signup` (`[auth.hook.before_user_created]`, local and production) refuses every new account that comes from an e-mail or a phone number. Without it, anyone could create an account for someone else's address with a password they know, and keep access to it once the owner signs in by code. The signup settings stay open: turning off `enable_signup` blocks the anonymous accounts, turning off the e-mail one blocks the sign-in by code.
- `delete-account` takes the account from the verified token alone, never from the request, erases the files of the account in `photos`, `alto` and `models` before the account, then `alto` once more (an A that ended meanwhile; a failure there is logged as `account_files_left`, the deletion stands), and logs no identifier.
- `transfer-account` takes both accounts from the two verified tokens alone (the first must be anonymous, the second registered), never from the request. It copies the photos, moves the rows in one transaction (`transfer_account_rows`, which rewrites the paths of photos and ALTO files), copies the ALTO files (after the rows, so that an A written meanwhile is not left behind), then erases the old files of the three buckets and the anonymous account; every step can be played again. It refuses while a page of the anonymous account is being read, is never stopped by a usage limit, and logs no identifier.
